## In one line

Slate is the control plane for autonomous marketing — the layer where a marketing org sets a goal and agents remember, watch, and ship the work to hit it. Ahrefs owns the data layer. Zapier owns the execution layer. **Slate owns the layer where marketing actually thinks.**

## Why this is worth a look now

Three things rarely line up at once. Here they do:

1. **A budget is moving, not just a market that exists.** ~$60B of global marketing-operations spend is shifting from human seats to agent seats by 2028. The average marketing team is already ~40% smaller than in 2025, and that budget moved to tooling and has not moved back. This is a re-allocation of dollars that are already being spent — not a category that has to be created from zero.

2. **The moat moved to exactly what Slate owns.** As frontier models converge in raw reasoning, the durable advantage is no longer the model — it's memory, context, and integrations. Slate is built natively around that: a compounding memory graph that gets more valuable every day it's used, and only for the customer who used it.

3. **The buyer changed.** The generation now running marketing expects an agent that does the work, not an analyst that hands them a dashboard. "AI-native marketer" went from a niche title to a standard job req in 24 months. Every team is already trying to make Claude their coworker by hand — Slate is the product they're improvising toward.

## The problem Slate solves

Every CMO has been handed the same impossible math: 10x the output with half the team. The tools they were given can't get them there because each one owns only a fragment of the job:

- **Ahrefs / Semrush** surface data, then stop. An alert fires and nothing happens.
- **Claude / ChatGPT projects** reason beautifully but forget — no outcome memory, no team-wide context.
- **Zapier / n8n** execute workflows but have no marketing judgment. Pipes, not brains.

No tool owns the loop from *signal → shipped work*. That loop is the entire job of a marketing team, and it's the gap Slate fills.

## What Slate is

A single goal input replaces eight dashboards. Underneath it:

- **Agentic watchers that never sleep** — background workers on rankings, pages, competitors, and AI citations. They create the work; no one has to ask.
- **Native AI-search visibility** — share of voice across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and AI Overviews, plus the ability to fix the pages AI can't cite. This is timely: organic clicks dropped ~57% after AI Overviews, and ChatGPT converts ~4x better than Google organic from homepage visits. The channel has already shifted; Slate is built for where it went.
- **A company brain any agent inherits** — point Claude Code, Codex, or a custom agent at Slate and it instantly gets the company's full marketing context.

## The moat: context compounds

This is the part that matters most for a company that needs to become durable, not just big.

Every campaign, edit, approval, and rejection feeds back into the memory graph. Day 300 is a materially better colleague than day 1 — **and only for that customer.** On top of that, 500+ campaign playbooks are baked in on day one, and every new customer's outcomes sharpen the shared defaults. The result is a flywheel — *memory → intelligence → signals → agents → memory* — where switching cost rises every single day the product is used. Even a competitor's agent gets better the moment it plugs into Slate, which turns rivals into distribution.

## Business model — three compounding layers

1. **Base recurring · ~$18K/yr avg** — coworker seats + control plane. Predictable, annual ARR.
2. **High-margin expansion · ~$6K avg additional** — skill packs (Paid, CRO, PR outreach). Same customer, more surface.
3. **Infrastructure, long-term · $5–10K/yr per external agent** — bring-your-own-agent monetization. Anyone pointing Claude Code, Codex, or a custom agent at Slate pays for the plane underneath.

Each new product area reuses the memory, integrations, and governance the last one earned — so every expansion is cheaper to build and sell than the one before.

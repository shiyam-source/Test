Technical audit · Webflow → Sanity

staging.tripledart.com against www.tripledart.com

A pre-launch audit of the new Sanity build (staging) compared with the current Webflow site (live). It covers page-level Core Web Vitals, mobile responsiveness, migration completeness, and what still has to happen before the domain switches.

551 sitemap URLs crawled on both 10 page templates 100 Lighthouse runs 5 viewports × 2 sites 10 Nov 2026

Verdict: the right move, but not ready to launch.

The Sanity build is the better platform. Its content templates (blog, case studies, research, coach) load faster, every page runs less JavaScript, and staging wins the desktop performance score on {{desktop_perf_W}} of 10 templates. The content migrated almost perfectly: all 551 URLs resolve with matching titles, descriptions, H1s and canonicals.

Launching today would cost leads, attribution and rankings. HubSpot forms are gone from 85 pages, the HubSpot, GTM and Cookiebot tags are missing sitewide, there is no XML sitemap, and the homepage, service and industry pages are 1.5–2.3s slower to show their hero on mobile. Each of these can be fixed. Fix the P0 list below, re-measure, then switch DNS.

Why hero pages regressed

On the homepage, service, industry and about pages, the LCP element is the hero headline. Its CSS starts it at opacity: 0.001, and it only appears once GSAP or webflow.js runs the entry animation. That JavaScript can only run after first render, and on staging first render waits for two render-blocking stylesheets: the 112KB tripledart.min.css (~1.2s) and Swiper's CSS from cdn.jsdelivr.net (~0.9s, which also needs a new connection). Webflow avoids the delay by sending both through 103 Early Hints and preloads, so the live hero appears ~1.5–2.3s sooner.

Why content pages improved

Blog posts, case studies, research and the listing pages no longer wait on Webflow's runtime chunks and jQuery from its CDN, and they ship smaller, properly sized Sanity images (auto=format, more srcsets). The blog post's mobile LCP went from 7.1s to 3.5s, and the blog index transfers 0.8MB instead of 3.4MB. Total blocking time is lower on {{mobile_tbt_W}} of 10 templates, which points to better INP for real users.

These numbers isolate the platform: ad, analytics, consent and visitor-ID scripts were blocked on both sites. Live currently loads about 25 third-party scripts against about 6 on staging, so real-user numbers will move once tracking is re-added (see P0). No field (CrUX) data was collected, because PageSpeed Insights needs an API key this audit could not use.

Launch checklist

What to fix, in order

P0

HubSpot forms removed from 85 pages

The calculators, playbooks, service pages and event pages lost their js.hsforms.net/forms/v2.js loader and hbspt.forms.create embed (for example /tools/acv-calculator, form 4d3453aa…). Gated content shows no form, so these pages capture no leads.

Re-add the embeds as a Sanity "HubSpot form" block keyed by form ID, then submit a test lead on each template.

P0

Every form submission is blocked, and Webflow forms will die with the Webflow plan

A sitewide script calls preventDefault() on every submit unless the form has data-td-local, which is fine as a staging guard. The newsletter and "get this guide" forms on 400 pages are still Webflow-native and post to webflow.com/api/v1/form/<siteId>, which stops accepting submissions once the Webflow site is unpublished.

Move these forms to HubSpot (or a Cloudflare Worker that writes to HubSpot), and remove the submit guard in the production build.

P0

Tracking and consent stack missing

Missing on staging: GTM GTM-N8NS3MP (all pages) and GTM-5BMJZCMD (84 pages), HubSpot tracking 20036758, Cookiebot, and the dataLayer listeners for hubspot-form-success, formsubmit_event and calendly. Without them, LinkedIn Insight, the Meta pixel, Clarity, Leadfeeder, Apollo, Warmly and the other visitor-ID tools stop firing, and the Ads and GA4 conversion events disappear.

Port the Webflow head and footer custom code into the site layout, in order: Cookiebot, then GTM, then HubSpot, then the listeners. Check with GTM Preview and HubSpot's source report.

P0

Staging is writing into production analytics without consent

Cloudflare's tag gateway (/gjbe/) on staging already sends GA4 G-8HV2B4EJMJ and Google Ads AW-11267922091 hits from staging.tripledart.com, with no consent banner. Test traffic is inflating production reports and remarketing lists.

Turn the gateway off for the staging hostname, or filter it out in GA4, until launch.

P0

Indexing switches: noindex, robots.txt, sitemap

All staging pages send X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow plus a robots meta tag, and robots.txt disallows everyone except Googlebot and Bingbot. Staging has no /sitemap.xml. Going live with any of these left in place de-indexes the site.

Make all three environment-driven. Generate /sitemap.xml from Sanity, leaving out /marketing/b2b-saas-marketing-agency (noindex on live) and including the 44 /case-studies/* and ~10 /tools/* pages the live sitemap misses. Restore the live robots.txt and submit the sitemap in Search Console on launch day.

P0

Mobile LCP regression on hero pages (+1.5–2.3s)

The homepage (3.6 → 5.1s), service (5.1 → 7.2s), industry (3.2 → 5.3s) and about (3.8 → 5.7s) pages are slower because of render-blocking CSS combined with a JavaScript-revealed hero. These are your highest-intent landing pages.

Self-host or inline Swiper's CSS, inline the above-the-fold CSS and load tripledart.min.css without blocking, send Link: rel=preload headers so Cloudflare emits 103 Early Hints, and render the hero headline visible by default (animate from a visible state, not from opacity 0).

P1

Content drift: one CMS field unmapped, and edits made in Webflow after the snapshot

On about 160 blog posts the hero subtitle differs. Live uses a dedicated hero field (for example /blog/b2b-ppc: "Maximize your B2B PPC ROI. Let TripleDart manage your campaigns."), while staging renders the post summary in that spot. Webflow is still being published (last publish 10 Nov), so changes keep landing there: the pricing FAQ on /book-meeting and /fractional-cmo/* differs, and the three newest research reports are missing from the related cards on 17 research pages.

Map the Webflow hero-subtitle field into Sanity and re-run the import for it. Freeze Webflow edits from now until launch (or log them), then do a final delta sync and re-run this text diff.

P1

Static assets are never cached by the browser

CSS, JS, fonts and images under /webflow/* and /base.css are served with Cache-Control: public, max-age=0, must-revalidate, so every repeat page view revalidates every file. Live caches them for about 24 hours.

Fingerprint the filenames and serve public, max-age=31536000, immutable through a _headers rule. Keep HTML at max-age=0.

P1

Broken JSON-LD on 18 pages

16 blog posts (for example /blog/aeo-checklist and /blog/top-abm-agencies) plus /google-analytics-4-consulting-services and /saas-performance-marketing-agency put several JSON objects in one <script type="application/ld+json">. That's invalid JSON, so Google drops all of their BlogPosting, FAQ, Breadcrumb and HowTo markup. On live, the second service page is also broken, by a raw control character.

Emit one script tag per object, or wrap them in an @graph array. Validate in the build.

P1

Missing page, missing llms.txt, soft duplicates

/coach/how-to-approach-brand-mention-building-to-improve-geo is live (200) and linked internally but returns 404 on staging. /llms.txt (6KB on live) returns 404, which matters for an AEO/GEO agency. /home returns a 200 duplicate of the homepage.

Migrate the coach entry, publish llms.txt (ideally generated from Sanity), and 301 /home to /.

P1

Trailing-slash redirects are 307, not 301

/about/ → /about returns a temporary 307 on staging, where live returns a 301. A temporary redirect doesn't consolidate signals as reliably.

Switch to a 301 or 308 in the Cloudflare redirect rule.

P1

Images point at the Sanity "staging" dataset

All ~3,900 image and OG references (project p18mi3o7) are served from /images/p18mi3o7/staging/…. If that dataset is renamed, cleaned or replaced after launch, every image breaks.

Decide now: promote the content to a production dataset before launch and rebuild, or formally keep staging as the live dataset and protect it.

P1

Security headers dropped

Live sends HSTS, X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff, X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN and frame-ancestors 'self'. Staging sends none of them, and its HTML has no charset in Content-Type.

Add them to _headers, along with Content-Type: text/html; charset=utf-8.

P2

Smaller gaps

og:url is missing on 467 pages. The 11 author pages use the share title "TripleDart 2.0". /blog is 974KB of HTML with 559 images (live: 197KB and 41), so consider paginating it. The calculator pages load a render-blocking old-site.scoped.css (~1.7s). Brand font preloads use .otf; switch to woff2. Two Webflow CDN asset links remain (/blog/outsourcing-b2b-marketing, /saas-paid-social-agency).

P2

Carried over from Webflow (unchanged, worth fixing while you're in there)

26 pages have no meta description (author pages, several playbooks), 10 pages have multiple H1s, 2 have none (/ai-instructions, /llm-info), 183 have no structured data, and 173 still have no OG image. About 34k of 36k images are lazy-loaded with no width and height attributes, including some above the fold, and colour contrast and ARIA roles fail Lighthouse audits on both sites.

Webflow → Sanity specifics

Easy-to-miss items when leaving Webflow

Before DNS switch

  • Export Webflow's 301 list (Site settings → Publishing → 301 redirects) and replay every row against staging. This audit only sampled them.
  • Export the custom code from site settings (head and footer) and every page's custom code. That's where GTM, HubSpot, Cookiebot and the listeners lived.
  • Download Webflow form submission history, and repoint any Zapier or Make zaps that use Webflow form triggers.
  • Find every PDF or file linked from cdn.prod.website-files.com in content or emails, and re-host the ones that are still in use.
  • Lower the DNS TTL a day ahead, check the Cloudflare SSL mode, and make sure tripledart.com 301s to www (it does on live today).
  • Note any Search Console or Bing verification that relies on Webflow-hosted files or tags. Neither site uses meta tags, so DNS verification will carry over.

Sanity / build pipeline

  • Publish webhook → rebuild: measure how long a content edit takes to go live, and set up preview for drafts.
  • Scheduled publishing for blog posts, which Webflow handled natively.
  • Editor roles and SSO, plus dataset backups or exports on a schedule (Sanity doesn't version-control deleted documents forever).
  • Sanity image CDN bandwidth and plan limits, since every page now pulls from cdn.sanity.io.
  • A rich-text block for each Webflow embed type in posts: HubSpot forms, Calendly, YouTube and Wistia (48 and 6 pages), tables (174 pages), and code embeds.
  • An automated check in CI for JSON-LD validity, a noindex leak, a missing canonical and sitemap generation.

Launch day and the following weeks

  • Submit the sitemap, then watch the Search Console Pages report and 404s daily for two weeks.
  • Add an annotation in GA4, compare conversions by form ID week over week, and check the HubSpot "original source" mix.
  • Turn on Cloudflare Web Analytics or a RUM tool so you get real CWV for the new stack before CrUX's 28-day window catches up.
  • Re-run this audit against www after launch, with tracking restored, to confirm the third-party cost.
  • Keep the Webflow site published on a staging subdomain for 30 days as a rollback.
Method

How this was measured

  • Crawl: all 554 URLs from the live /sitemap.xml (551 unique paths) fetched on both hosts, with no redirects followed. Compared fields were status, title, description, robots, canonical, OG, H1, schema types, images, internal links and word count. A further 61 internally linked URLs that aren't in the sitemap were also checked.
  • Embeds and tags: a regex scan of every page's HTML on both hosts for HubSpot, GTM, Cookiebot, Calendly, iframes, video, Lottie and forms, plus a headless render to see which tags actually fire.
  • Performance: Lighthouse 12 on headless Chromium. Mobile used the default Moto G Power, slow-4G simulated throttle (3 runs); desktop used the desktop preset (2 runs). Medians are reported. Third-party marketing tags were blocked on both sites to compare the platforms like for like. Lab data, not field data.
  • Responsive: Playwright at 360/390/768 (touch) and 1024/1440 widths. Each page was scrolled fully, measured for overflow, broken images, tap targets under 24px and text under 12px, and its above-the-fold screenshot pixel-diffed against live.
  • Not covered: real-user CrUX data (no API key), a full replay of Webflow's redirect list (needs the export), and actual form submission tests (staging blocks submits by design).
Audit run 10 Nov 2026 from a US-East cloud container. The figures are lab measurements; expect variance of about ±10% between runs.

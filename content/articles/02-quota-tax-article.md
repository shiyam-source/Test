# The Quota Tax: what AI visibility tracking really costs after Profound's pricing change

In September 2026, the week Profound announced a $180M Series D at a $1.8B valuation, its self-serve plans for brands reportedly disappeared. The $99 Starter and $399 Growth tiers had been the way a mid-market team could buy Profound without a sales call. Now a brand gets a 7-day trial and then a custom Enterprise quote. Third parties estimate those quotes at $2,000 to $5,000+ a month, and a secondhand Vendr figure puts the median contract around $34,500 a year.

Most of the commentary has been about the sticker price. We think the more useful question sits underneath it: **what does a quota actually buy you?**

We normalized eleven plans across the market four different ways. The headline: the price on the page is the smallest cost of an AI visibility quota. The bigger costs are paid in time and in clients, and almost nobody prices them. We call that the quota tax.

The full analysis is **The Quota Tax** ([download the PDF](#)). Here's the short version.

## How Profound's pricing got here

| When | What a brand could buy |
|---|---|
| June 2025 | "Profound Lite", $499/month self-serve, launched with the Series A |
| Early to mid 2026 | Starter $99 (50 prompts, ChatGPT only) and Growth $399 (100 prompts, 3 engines), annual billing only |
| Mid-September 2026 | Brand self-serve plans reportedly withdrawn |
| November 2026 | 7-day trial, then custom Enterprise |

Agencies can still buy self-serve: $99 a month base plus $399 per client workspace, each covering 100 prompts on three engines, run daily. Claude, Gemini and Copilot tracking are reported as Enterprise-only.

None of this makes Profound a bad product. It has more capital than the rest of the category combined, strong enterprise logos, panel-based prompt data and an agent roadmap moving fast. But if you're a 200-person SaaS company, there's no longer a published price between "try it for a week" and "talk to sales."

## Unit 1: price per prompt (the one everyone quotes)

At list price, per tracked prompt per month:

| Plan | $ / prompt / month |
|---|---|
| Scrunch Starter | $0.86 |
| Peec Pro | $1.63 |
| Otterly Standard | $1.89 |
| Peec Starter | $1.90 |
| Otterly Lite | $1.93 |
| Profound Starter (removed) | $1.98 |
| Profound Growth (removed) / agency workspace | **$3.99** |

Profound's 100-prompt tier was the most expensive per prompt in the market. That matches what reviewers flagged on G2: "limited prompt availability for the price."

The trouble with this unit is that it hides everything that matters: how many engines, how many runs, and whether you can choose. A prompt run once a day on one engine and a prompt run ten times a day on four engines both count as "one prompt."

## Unit 2: price per answer (the real unit of evidence)

Each AI answer is one piece of evidence. Normalize to that and the ranking changes:

| Plan | $ / sampled answer |
|---|---|
| Slate Growth, net of bundled credits | $0.012 |
| Peec Pro | $0.018 |
| Ahrefs custom prompts | $0.020 |
| Peec Starter | $0.021 |
| Profound Growth / agency workspace | $0.044 |
| **Slate Growth (gross)** | **$0.050** |
| Profound Starter (removed) | $0.066 |
| AthenaHQ Starter | $0.082 |

Note that Slate isn't the cheapest per answer at list price. At $0.050, Slate Growth costs slightly more per answer than Profound's $0.044. The case for Slate is what else the same spend includes: unlimited prompts, a fourth engine (Google AI Mode), 1,000 monitored pages, and 15,000 execution credits for content, audits and fixes. Valued at Slate's own top-up rate, those credits bring tracking down to about $0.012 per answer. A comparison that quotes a per-prompt price for a credit-based plan is comparing different things.

## Unit 3: price per trustworthy reading (it's small everywhere)

Here's where it gets interesting. A reading you can act on needs enough answers behind it. At a 30% appearance rate:

- ±10 points needs **81 answers**
- ±5 points needs **323 answers**

(Our companion study, The Coin-Flip Report, shows why: a single prompt sampled daily for a month can read anywhere from 17% to 48% when the truth is 30%.)

Multiply by price per answer and a decision-grade reading on one prompt costs **$1 to $7 for ±10 points**, or **$4 to $26 for ±5 points**, on any plan in the market.

So money isn't what limits you.

## Unit 4: time to confidence (the actual quota tax)

What limits you is cadence. If a plan runs each prompt once a day per engine, that prompt collects 30 answers a month on that engine, whatever you'd be willing to pay for more.

Time to reach a ±5-point reading on one prompt:

| Sampling design | Time to ±5 points |
|---|---|
| 1 prompt, 1 engine, once a day | **10.8 months** |
| 1 prompt, 3 engines pooled, once a day | 3.6 months |
| 1 prompt, 4 engines, 3 runs a day | about 27 days |
| 10-prompt topic, 3 engines, once a day | about 11 days |

That's the quota tax. A team that can't move sampling depth onto the prompts it's fixing waits months to learn whether a fix worked, and acts on noise in the meantime. Ship a new comparison page in January and a fixed daily cadence won't give you a trustworthy per-prompt answer until spring.

Two things shorten the wait without a bigger contract: **trend topics instead of single prompts**, and **use a plan that lets you set runs per prompt**, so priority prompts get depth and background prompts don't eat the budget.

## The agency version: per-workspace pricing grows with every client

For agencies and multi-brand companies, the quota tax is charged per brand. On reported figures, Profound's agency pricing works out to:

| Clients | Monthly cost on Profound (agency) |
|---|---|
| 1 | $498 |
| 5 | $2,094 |
| 10 | **$4,089** |
| 20 | $8,079 |
| 30 | $12,069 |

That's before any client gets Claude or Gemini coverage. Three consequences follow:

1. **Small retainers get under-tracked.** When every client costs $399 in software, a $2,000/month retainer either absorbs a 20% tool cost or gets dropped from tracking.
2. **Engine choices get made by budget.** If extra engines sit behind an Enterprise contract, an agency picks engines per client based on cost, not on where that client's buyers actually search.
3. **The vendor's AI products overlap with the agency's work.** Profound pays certified agency partners while marketing an "AI Marketer" that finds earned-media opportunities and refreshes stale content. Before building a practice on any platform, agencies should know where that line sits.

Slate's Agency plan is reported at $2,000 a month flat, which would come out cheaper than per-workspace pricing at about five clients, *if* its allowances cover your book. Ask for the allowances in writing before you do that math.

## The engine tax

Buyers increasingly want Claude and Gemini tracked alongside ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google. What it costs to add them:

- **Profound:** reported as Enterprise-only (third-party estimates start around $2,000/month)
- **Slate:** $100/month add-on, published
- **Ahrefs Brand Radar:** $199 per AI platform index, or $699 for all
- **Otterly:** paid add-ons for Claude, AI Mode and Gemini

The engines a buyer wants most are the ones most often gated.

## Twelve questions to ask before you sign anything

1. How many answers per prompt, per engine, per month does the plan collect? Get it in writing.
2. Is the run cadence fixed, or can I put more runs on priority prompts?
3. Can I group prompts into topics and trend the topic?
4. Which engines are included, and what does each additional engine cost, published or quoted?
5. What happens at the quota: hard stop, overage charge, or forced upgrade?
6. Monthly or annual-only? What are the auto-renewal and notice terms?
7. For agencies: per-workspace pricing or a pooled allowance? Is white-label reporting included?
8. Does trial data carry over? Can I export raw answers?
9. Who owns my prompt history and answer archive if I leave?
10. Does the price include doing the work (content, corrections, outreach), or only measuring it?
11. API or consumer app collection? From which location?
12. What's the smallest change your reports treat as real?

Then normalize every quote to price per answer and work out how long your top 20 prompts take to reach ±10 points under the vendor's cadence. If the answer is "a quarter," you'll be reviewing noise for a quarter.

## Where this leaves a mid-market team

If you're comparing Profound and Slate today, the useful comparison isn't $99 against $499. That $99 plan reportedly no longer exists for brands. It's this:

- **What can I buy without a sales cycle?** Profound brands: a 7-day trial. Slate: published self-serve plans from $499/month.
- **What does the spend include?** Profound: tracking, plus agents on higher tiers. Slate: tracking plus execution credits, with TripleDart operators available when you want the work done for you.
- **How fast can I trust a change?** That depends on whether you can put sampling depth where the work is. Ask both vendors question 2.
- **What does it cost to add engines and brands?** Ask both vendors questions 4 and 7, and get the answers in writing.

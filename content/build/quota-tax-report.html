Executive summary

The price on the page is the smallest cost of a quota

AI visibility tools are compared on price per prompt. That is the least informative unit available. Prices per answer differ by about 4x across the market. Two other costs are much larger: how long a fixed cadence makes you wait before a number can be trusted, and how per-workspace pricing grows with every client or brand you add. We call these the quota tax.

Key findings

  • Price per answer ranges from about $0.02 to $0.08. A decision-grade reading on one prompt (±10 points) costs between $1 and $7 in answers on any plan. The dollars are not what limits you.
  • Cadence is what limits you. With a fixed once-daily run, one prompt-engine collects 30 answers a month, regardless of budget. Faster certainty comes from pooling engines, grouping prompts into topics, or buying more runs on the prompts that matter.
  • Per-workspace pricing grows linearly with clients. On reported figures, an agency pays $99 plus $399 for each client on Profound. A flat agency plan becomes cheaper at about five clients, if its allowances cover the book.
  • Extra engines cost most where they matter most. Reports say Claude, Gemini and Copilot tracking require a Profound Enterprise contract. Slate lists them as a $100/month add-on.†

Context

Units 1 and 2

Price per prompt and price per answer rank vendors differently

Slate is not the cheapest per answer at list price. At $0.050 per answer, Slate Growth costs slightly more than Profound's $0.044. Slate's case rests on what else the spend buys: unlimited prompts, a fourth engine (Google AI Mode), 1,000 monitored pages, and execution credits for content, audits and fixes. Net of those credits, tracking costs about $0.012 per answer. Any comparison that quotes only a per-prompt price for a credit-based plan is comparing different things.

Units 3 and 4

A trustworthy number costs a few dollars and several months

A reading you can act on needs enough answers. At a 30% appearance rate, ±10 points needs 81 answers and ±5 points needs 323 (see The Coin-Flip Report). The dollar cost of those answers is small on every plan:

The constraint is time. A plan that runs each prompt once a day collects 30 answers a month for that prompt on that engine, no matter how much the buyer would pay for more. The only ways to get a confident reading faster are pooling (several engines, or several prompts in one topic) or more runs per day on the prompts that matter.

The quota tax, definedThe cost of a fixed quota is mostly paid in time. A team that can't move sampling depth onto the prompts it is fixing waits months to learn whether the fix worked, and spends that time acting on noise.
Agencies and multi-brand teams

Per-workspace pricing grows with every client

Agencies and multi-brand companies pay the quota tax per brand. On reported figures, Profound's agency pricing is $99 a month plus $399 per client workspace, each covering 100 prompts on three engines, run daily.† Ten clients cost $4,089 a month; twenty cost $8,079; thirty cost $12,069. Claude and Gemini coverage is not included at that price.

Three things per-client pricing changes

  1. Small retainers get under-tracked. When every client costs $399 in software, a $2,000 retainer either absorbs a 20% tool cost or gets dropped from tracking.
  2. Engine choices are made per client. If extra engines sit behind Enterprise, the agency decides by budget, not by where each client's buyers search.
  3. The vendor's AI products compete with the agency's work. Profound pays certified agency partners while marketing an "AI Marketer" that finds earned-media opportunities and refreshes content. Agencies should check where that line falls before building a practice on it.
For buyers

The quota checklist: twelve questions before you sign

  1. How many answers per prompt, per engine, per month does the plan collect? Get it in writing.
  2. Is the run cadence fixed, or can I put more runs on priority prompts?
  3. Can I group prompts into topics and trend the topic?
  4. Which engines are included, and what does each additional engine cost, published or quoted?
  5. What happens at the quota: hard stop, overage charge, or forced upgrade?
  6. Is billing monthly or annual-only? What are the auto-renewal and notice terms?
  7. For agencies: is pricing per workspace or per pooled allowance? Is white-label reporting included?
  8. Does trial data carry over into the paid plan? Can I export raw answers?
  9. Who owns my prompt history and answer archive if I leave?
  10. Does the price include doing the work (content, corrections, outreach), or only the measurement?
  11. Is collection by API or from the consumer app, and from which location?
  12. What is the smallest change your reports treat as real?
How to use thisNormalize every quote to price per answer, then work out how long your top 20 prompts take to reach ±10 points under the vendor's cadence. If the answer is "a quarter," you will be reviewing noise for a quarter.
Methodology and sources

How prices were normalized

All prices are monthly list prices in USD as reported between September and November 2026. Answers per month = prompts × engines × 30 daily runs, unless a plan states an answer or credit allowance. Profound's Enterprise pricing is unpublished; third-party estimates are shown as estimates, never as list prices. Slate's net-of-credits figure values bundled credits at the published top-up rate and is shown alongside the gross figure. Decision-grade readings use a 30% baseline and normal-approximation sample sizes (81 for ±10 points, 323 for ±5 points).

ChatGPT does it in 25%. It reaches for site: instead.

When a model answers with search, it doesn't search your prompt. It rewrites it into several sub-queries (fan-out) and retrieves for those. The rewrites are what your page actually has to match.

So we looked at which modifiers each model adds. [__] prompts, [__] fan-out queries, ChatGPT and three Claude models.

Share of fan-out queries containing each modifier:

Year → ChatGPT 24.6% · Haiku 4.5 22.6% · Sonnet 5 35.7% · Opus 5 57.4%
"best" → 10.1% · 18.2% · 23.7% · 3.3%
"comparison" → 0.8% · 5.5% · 9.8% · 3.8%
site: → 20.4% in ChatGPT. Not in the top 100 for any Claude model.
"official" → 13.6% in ChatGPT. Same.

Four things stand out.

𝗙𝗿𝗲𝘀𝗵𝗻𝗲𝘀𝘀 𝗶𝘀 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗱𝗼𝗺𝗶𝗻𝗮𝗻𝘁 𝗿𝗲𝘄𝗿𝗶𝘁𝗲.
Year is the #1 modifier in every model, and in the Claude family it scales with size: 23% → 36% → 57%. My read: larger models know their training data is stale, so they pin the search to the present. A query with "2026" in it pulls toward pages that show 2026. In seven weeks those queries say 2027. Most pages won't.

𝗖𝗵𝗮𝘁𝗚𝗣𝗧 𝗴𝗼𝗲𝘀 𝘀𝘁𝗿𝗮𝗶𝗴𝗵𝘁 𝘁𝗼 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝘀𝗼𝘂𝗿𝗰𝗲.
1 in 5 of its searches uses site:, and 1 in 7 says "official". It picks the domain that should hold the answer, then searches inside it. If your own pages don't answer a site: query on your own domain, the model falls back to someone else describing you.

𝗢𝗽𝘂𝘀 𝘀𝗲𝗮𝗿𝗰𝗵𝗲𝘀 𝗹𝗲𝗮𝗻.
After the year, nothing in Opus 5 clears 4%. "best" collapses from 23.7% in Sonnet 5 to 3.3%. Haiku and Sonnet write queries that look like a buyer's search. Opus writes queries that look like a researcher's.

𝗧𝗵𝗲𝗿𝗲 𝗶𝘀 𝗻𝗼 𝘀𝗶𝗻𝗴𝗹𝗲 "𝗔𝗜 𝗾𝘂𝗲𝗿𝘆".
"comparison" goes from 0.8% in ChatGPT to 9.8% in Sonnet 5. "top" isn't in ChatGPT's top 100 at all. Optimising for "the" fan-out means optimising for one model.

What I'd do with this:
1. Put the year where retrieval sees it: title, H1, visible date, dateModified. Update before 1 January, not after.
2. Run site: queries on your own domain for your core questions, in Bing and Google. If the answer isn't there, the model won't find it either.
3. Build prompt sets from more than one model's fan-out. One engine's queries give you a one-sided map.

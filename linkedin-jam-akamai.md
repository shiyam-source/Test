Akamai built a second version of its website. Only AI agents can see it.

The result: 364% more AI visibility on questions that never mentioned Akamai by name.

Here's how it works 👇

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗯𝗹𝗲𝗺
Akamai's site is huge: cybersecurity, cloud, developer infrastructure. But when people asked AI about the products Akamai sells, Akamai often didn't show up.

Their theory: AI agents weren't reading the site the way humans do. The JavaScript, menus and interactive elements that help a person just get in the way of a model trying to pull out facts.

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗳𝗶𝘅
→ Same URL, two versions.
Humans get the normal site. Supported AI agents get a stripped-down HTML version with the same product information and none of the clutter.

→ It runs at the CDN.
Akamai detects AI traffic at the edge, before it reaches the site, and routes it through Scrunch's Agent Experience Platform, which serves the simplified pages.

→ No redesign, no rewrite.
The machine-readable versions are generated from existing product pages and docs.

→ Not llms.txt.
Major AI search engines don't reliably read llms.txt. This serves the clean version at the moment an agent requests the page.

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝘁𝗲𝘀𝘁
They started small, with just the Inference Cloud pages. They measured visibility across a set of AI prompts, then compared optimized pages against untouched ones.

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗿𝗲𝘀𝘂𝗹𝘁𝘀
• 85% more AI citations
• Cited across 38% more unique prompts
• 364% more brand visibility on non-branded questions
• 133% better brand presence vs. competitors in ChatGPT

The third number is the one that matters.

Showing up when someone asks "what is Akamai?" is easy. Showing up when a buyer asks "what's the best cloud for low-latency AI inference worldwide?" is pipeline. (That's the exact prompt in the screenshot below.)

Akamai has since rolled it out across more of the site and turned it into a product called AI Brand Presence.

𝗠𝘆 𝘁𝗮𝗸𝗲𝗮𝘄𝗮𝘆 𝗳𝗼𝗿 𝗕𝟮𝗕 𝗺𝗮𝗿𝗸𝗲𝘁𝗲𝗿𝘀
Most AEO advice is about what you write. This is about how you deliver it. If your best content sits behind heavy JavaScript, AI may not be reading much of it.

One caveat: serving bots something different from humans has a messy history in SEO (it's called cloaking). The line Akamai held was the same information, less code. Stay on that side and it's a delivery upgrade. Cross it and it's a risk.

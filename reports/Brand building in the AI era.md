# Win attention with proof AI can't fake

Shiyam's LinkedIn reach fell mainly because the platform changed for everyone. Median creator reach is down somewhere between a third and 60% since 2024 ([AuthoredUp via Contentin](https://contentin.io/blog/linkedin-content-statistics/); [Creator Science #307](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/richard-van-der-blom-2/)). Three TripleDart habits are making it worse, and all three are fixable. First, the team runs an internal engagement pod, and LinkedIn says publicly it wants to make pods "entirely ineffective." Second, Shiyam posts across five topics and two brands, right after a repositioning, on a feed that now matches posts to readers by topic. Third, the content volume (8 SEO blogs a week, many parallel series) looks like what any AI harness now produces for free. More posting won't fix this, and neither will a new platform. Shiyam should narrow to one topic lane and publish only what TripleDart and Slate uniquely know: delivery data from 250+ B2B SaaS clients, the Slate Index, inside-out campaign teardowns, and live product proof. That content should then travel through people who have their own credibility: operators, clients, partner agencies, paid creators, Thought Leader Ads and small dinners. Colleagues asked to comment in the first hour don't count. LinkedIn stays the main stage for the next 12 months, because B2B buyers and AI answer engines both draw on it. Substack becomes the owned anchor and YouTube the second surface. Medium, X, Threads and Bluesky are minor or skip. The stakes are bigger than reach. Buyers pick from their day-one shortlist 95% of the time ([6sense](https://6sense.com/b2b-buyer-experience-report/)), and 51% now start software research in an AI chatbot ([G2](https://learn.g2.com/g2-2026-ai-search-insight-report)). The brand's job is to be remembered before the need arises and to be the source AI answers repeat. Over three years, "brand" will mean how models and peers describe TripleDart and Slate. That is built from third-party mentions and an owned audience, and follower counts are a poor proxy for it.

*How to read this report.* Linked figures are data. Almost all of it comes from vendors or surveys with commercial interests, and many figures were read from search summaries because the source sites were blocked (see section 8). Sentences labelled **inference**, and all recommendations, are judgment applied to Shiyam's situation. Facts about TripleDart's internal practices come from its Slack and Notion and are taken as given.

## 1. The diagnosis: reach fell for five reasons, and four are self-inflicted

**The platform-wide drop is real, and it explains most of the decline.** Every large dataset shows per-post reach falling sharply since 2024. In AuthoredUp's analysis of 621,833 posts, average reach was **down 34%, and 98% of users lost reach** while the top 2% gained ([AuthoredUp via Contentin](https://contentin.io/blog/linkedin-content-statistics/)). Richard van der Blom's 2026 dataset (about 1.3M posts) puts the drop at **about 60% over two years for active creators** ([Creator Science #307](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/richard-van-der-blom-2/)). Company pages fared worst: organic reach is reportedly around 1% of followers ([van der Blom 2025, Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/984921783/Algorithm-Insights-Report-2025-chapter-1-Richard-Van-der-Blom)) and down 60–66% since 2024 ([Ordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/the-declining-reach-of-linkedin-company-pages)). LinkedIn's own engagement is still growing, with comments up 24% year on year ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-reports-increase-in-post-comments-video-posts-microsoft-q1-2026/804353)). That combination means more content is competing for the same feed slots, and the gains are going to a small top tier. **Inference:** if Shiyam's impressions are down 35–60% from 2024, he is roughly at the market median and the rest of this section explains why he didn't beat it. If they are down much more than that, the causes below are doing the extra damage. His analytics will show which (see section 8).

**The feed now decides who sees a post by topic, and his topic keeps changing.** In 2025–26 LinkedIn rebuilt feed retrieval around LLM-generated embeddings. A dual encoder now picks about 2,000 candidate posts per viewer, replacing the older network-graph sources ([ppc.land summary of LinkedIn Engineering](https://ppc.land/linkedin-rebuilds-its-feed-from-scratch-with-llms-and-gpu-powered-ranking/)). A transformer ranker tuned for time spent then orders them ([arXiv 2602.12354](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12354v1)). Secondary sources say retrieval reads the author's headline and profile as well as the post ([siela.in](https://siela.in/blog-linkedin-360brew-algorithm)). Shiyam's recent feed mixes agency news, Slate launches (Slate x Slack, Slate for Agencies, Slate Index), hiring, GTM engineering and AI search, and his stated identity changed in July 2026. **Inference:** the model has no stable picture of what Shiyam is about, so each post gets matched to a smaller, less certain interest audience. LinkedIn has not published topic-matching weights, so this is a mechanism-consistent explanation, not a measured one.

**The #socialmediaupdates habit is an engagement pod, and LinkedIn is engineering against exactly this pattern.** Gyanda Sachdeva, LinkedIn's VP of Product, said: "Our goal is to make engagement pods entirely ineffective." LinkedIn says it uses behavioural signals to detect inauthentic interaction, and it removes automated comments from the default comment view ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-outlines-more-measures-to-combat-engagement-pods/812290/)). A team that posts "please engage in the first hour" for nearly every company and founder post produces a clear signature: the same few dozen people react within minutes, whatever the topic. **Inference:** in the worst case, that engagement is detected and discounted. In the best case it still teaches the ranker that Shiyam's audience is TripleDart employees, so each new post is tested first on people who will never buy. The first-hour signal is also heavily polluted: about 80% of the comments van der Blom gets in the first five minutes are AI-written ([Creator Science #307](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/richard-van-der-blom-2/)), which gives LinkedIn every reason to discount reflexive early engagement. In fairness, early conversation does still matter ([meet-lea summary of van der Blom](https://meet-lea.com/en/blog/linkedin-algorithm-explained)), and a substantive comment from Akilan that adds data has real value. The problem is the standing request, not colleagues engaging.

**The content looks like what AI makes for free, which is the worst look for a company that sells AI marketing.** About 53.7% of long-form LinkedIn posts by influential profiles are now likely AI-written. In marketing and branding, **human posts performed 73% better** ([Originality.ai](https://originality.ai/blog/linkedin-ai-study-engagement)). LinkedIn trains models with former journalists to spot "authentic, useful" posts, and limits low-quality AI content to direct connections ([CNN via Style Magazine, 2026-10-19](http://stylemagazine.com/news/2026/oct/19/even-linkedin-is-getting-tired-of-your-ai-written-posts/)). Readers penalise perceived AI whoever wrote the piece: trust falls about 50% when readers *suspect* AI, even on human-written text ([PPC Land on Raptive](https://ppc.land/raptive-study-shows-ai-content-cuts-reader-trust-by-half/)). On the web, AI-written articles now outnumber human ones, but they "largely don't appear" in Google and ChatGPT results ([GPTZero/Graphite](https://gptzero.me/news/ai-written-web/)). **Inference:** 8 SEO blogs a week, plus templated LinkedIn posts, buys volume in exactly the category distribution systems discount. For Slate this is worse than for anyone else, because every generic post is evidence that AI marketing produces sameness.

**Too many motions are started and too few finished, and the goals measure the wrong thing.** TripleDart currently runs a thought leadership series, a teardown series, data studies, 16 Slate Academy videos, a planned flagship event, a year-end report and "big bet" LinkedIn concepts. The podcast had a target of 20 episodes and none were made in the tracked period. The 2026 target of 20,000 LinkedIn followers tracks a number the new feed barely uses: distribution is now interest-matched, and follower growth is down 42–59% across datasets ([Updately summary of van der Blom](https://updately.ai/blog/linkedin-organic-reach-drop-pipeline-2026)). The positioning is borrowed as well. "GTM engineering" is Clay's term, backed by Clay's money and its 90–108 partner agencies ([Yespress](https://yespress.io/clay); [LymLyt](https://lymlyt.pro/blog/who-invented-gtm-engineer/)). "Services as software" is a venture-capital thesis that many AI-native firms now use ([Foundation Capital](https://foundationcapital.com/?p=10007)). **Inference:** both work as descriptors and neither works as a brand. "A GTM engineering company" puts TripleDart on a list with every agency that builds on Clay.

## 2. What B2B readers reward on LinkedIn now, and what to stop

**Posts reach fewer people, but the people they reach pay more attention.** Business-page engagement *rate* rose 8% year on year to 5.20% ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/linkedin)), even as impressions fell 23% in 2025 ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/press-release-2026-social-media-study/)). The new ranker is tuned for time spent, so it favours formats people actually read. AuthoredUp's personal-profile data (March 2025 to February 2026) gives the clearest format ranking:

| Format (personal profiles) | Reach vs. average | Engagement vs. average | Implication for Shiyam |
|---|---|---|---|
| Document carousels | 1.39x | 1.30x | Default format for data drops and teardowns; only 4.88% of posts use it |
| Images | 1.20x | 1.33x | Charts from the Slate Index or client benchmarks |
| Text only | 1.07x | 0.78x | Fine for sharp operator takes, if long and specific |
| Video | 0.86x | 0.93x | Supply glut; not a LinkedIn reach play |
| Polls | 1.78x | 0.37x | Reach trap: impressions without attention |
| Articles | 0.69x | 0.44x | Skip; publish long-form on Substack |
| Reshares | 0.29x | 0.22x | Stop |

Source: [AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/best-performing-content-on-linkedin). These multipliers are relative to the average post, so they don't mean absolute reach is rising.

Length helps. Posts of 20+ sentences get 1.14x reach, and posts under five sentences get 0.64x ([AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/how-to-increase-engagement-on-linkedin)). On frequency the datasets disagree: van der Blom says 2–4 posts a week, AuthoredUp says 4–5 ([carousels-generator summary](https://carousels-generator.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2026-carousels)). **Three to four strong posts a week from Shiyam** sits inside both ranges. The evidence on links is mixed. Van der Blom measures an 18.8% median reach cut from one external link ([Neal Schaffer](https://newsletter.nealschaffer.com/posts/linkedin-links-cost-you-18-8-reach)), while Ordinal found personal profiles "largely immune" compared with company pages ([Ordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/the-declining-reach-of-linkedin-company-pages)). So use a link when it matters, and test before adopting the link-in-comments ritual. Video is a special case. LinkedIn is pushing it because it sells pre-roll ads on creator video through BrandLink ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/linkedin-debuts-brandlink/)), but business-page video views fell 36% year on year ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/linkedin)).

**Readers reward specifics they can't get elsewhere, from named people, about a narrow problem.** The evidence here is thinner than the format data. No rigorous dataset compares original data with personal stories or contrarian takes. What exists points one way. Decision-makers say they spend real time on thought leadership (64% over an hour a week, per secondary reporting), and 53% say strong thought leadership makes brand recognition matter less ([Demand Gen Report on Edelman–LinkedIn](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/news-brief/marketers-need-to-gear-content-to-hidden-b2b-buyers-edelman-linkedin/49901/)). Kyle Poyar calls LinkedIn his single best acquisition channel and cites Pylon, where it is the top pipeline source ([Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/guide-to-mastering-linkedin)). Storylane credits founder plus employee posting with more than 50% of pipeline (secondary: [Linkboost](https://www.linkboost.co/blog/how-founders-grew-linkedin-following-2026/)). The common pattern is a narrow ICP, concrete numbers, a consistent topic and team-wide posting. LinkedIn also now shapes AI answers. One study found it cited in 14.3% of ChatGPT Search responses, and Profound ranks it the top domain for B2B and software queries ([5W PR](https://www.5wpr.com/research/citation-source-audit-q1-2026/); [Contently](https://contently.com/2026/04/29/top-sources-llms-cite/)). **Inference:** a strong post now does two jobs. It reaches a buyer, and it becomes raw material for the answer that buyer's chatbot gives later.

**What to stop, and what replaces it:**

| Stop | Why | Replace with |
|---|---|---|
| "Please engage in the first hour" requests in #socialmediaupdates | LinkedIn targets pods; trains the ranker on the wrong audience | New channel rule (section 5): share only if you have something real to add, and DM the post to one person it helps |
| Hiring, celebration and every launch on Shiyam's profile | Dilutes his topic signal | Hiring from hiring managers; launches from the Slate team and partners; Shiyam posts the *data angle* on a launch, not the announcement |
| Launching from the company page first | Company-page reach is about 1% of followers | People-first launches; the page reshares later, for the record |
| Polls, reshares, LinkedIn Articles | Lowest engagement formats (table above) | Carousels, long text with numbers |
| Templated hooks, AI-generic phrasing, more than 3 hashtags | Suspected AI halves trust; posts with more than 3 hashtags reportedly do 71% worse ([socialpilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/?p=694640)) | Shiyam's own voice; Slate drafts, and Shiyam supplies the facts and the final edit |
| 8 informational SEO blogs a week | AI articles mostly don't surface; CTR on AI-Overview queries fell from 1.76% to 0.61% ([Dataslayer on Seer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/google-ai-overviews-the-end-of-traditional-ctr-and-how-to-adapt-in-2025)) | 2–3 a week of comparison, bottom-funnel and data-backed pages, each with one proprietary fact |
| 20,000-follower target | Followers barely drive distribution now | The metrics in section 7 |
| Starting new series before finishing old ones | Podcast: 20 targeted, 0 shipped | Five franchises with fixed cadences (section 4) |

Don't over-correct on SEO. Google still handles roughly 8x ChatGPT's query volume ([BrandGEO citing Ahrefs](https://brandgeo.co/blog/gartner-25-percent-search-drop-what-to-model)), and US organic traffic fell only 2.5% year on year by January 2026 ([Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/seo/ai-overview-impact-on-organic-search-2026/)). The loss falls on informational "what is X" content, which is exactly where most of the 8 posts a week go.

## 3. The channel portfolio: one stage, one owned anchor, one video surface

The Rand Fishkin line that "don't build on rented land is no longer viable advice" ([Near Media EP 244](https://nearmedia.co/ep-244-ai-visibility-vs-google-rankings-rand-fishkin-on-the-future-of-search-zero-click-brand-measurement)) and the case for owning an audience fit together. You build where attention already is (LinkedIn, YouTube, AI answers), and you convert that attention into something you own (subscribers, relationships, partners).

| Channel | Role | Effort | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** (Shiyam + 5–8 operators) | Main stage for B2B buyers; feeds AI answers | High (Shiyam ~4 hrs/wk on posts and comments) | **Core.** One topic lane, 3–4 posts/week, carousels with proprietary data, plus Thought Leader Ads on winners |
| **Substack** (Shiyam's personal publication) | Owned anchor; long-form home for the franchises | Medium-high (4–6 hrs/issue, weekly or biweekly) | **Start now.** The Recommendations network is the reason to pick it over beehiiv |
| **YouTube** (video conversations + Slate Academy) | Video home; AI-citation surface; buyer tutorials | Medium (outsourced editing) | **Build in H1 2027**, in a smaller form than the failed 20-episode plan |
| **Company email** (Slate Index data list on HubSpot/beehiiv) | Distribution for monthly Index drops to marketers, journalists and agencies | Low-medium | **Yes.** Keep it separate from Shiyam's personal voice |
| **AI-answer presence** (G2, third-party listicles, comparison pages) | Where shortlists form | Medium | **Core**, and Slate should run it on itself |
| **Events** (dinners, later one flagship) | Human validation; pipeline | Medium per event | **Dinners now**; a flagship only once dinners show demand |
| **Communities** (Pavilion, RevGenius, Slack groups; a Slate agency community) | Relationships and seeding | Low-medium | **Selective**; build your own around Slate for Agencies |
| **Reddit** | Practitioner trust; some AI citations | Low | **Hedge only**: genuine answers from Shiyam/Akilan, no promotion |
| **X** | AI-builder and investor audience | Low | **Optional**, mainly if Slate fundraises |
| **Medium** | Possible AI-citation surface | Very low | **Cross-post canonicals at most** |
| **Threads, Bluesky** | Consumer-led or shrinking | None | **Skip** |

**Substack wins on discovery.** Substack Recommendations brought Lenny Rachitsky 78% of his new free readers ([Growth in Reverse](https://growthinreverse.substack.com/p/the-5-biggest-takeaways-from-lennys)). About half of Kyle Poyar's new subscribers came the same way, via 500+ recommending publications, and he reached 78K subscribers and 4M annual views while holding another job ([Growth Unhinged](https://kylepoyar.substack.com/p/a-new-chapter)). The platform has 5M+ paid subscriptions and a $1.1B valuation ([BetaKit](https://betakit.com/canadian-co-founded-substack-raises-100-million-usd-series-c-to-grow-tools-for-independent-creators/)). It also has a catch that suits Shiyam. Tech is the most AI-saturated category, with 28% of top tech-newsletter text flagged as AI. Since August 2026 Substack has shown readers an AI-detection label ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/12/nx-s1-5919251/substacks-new-ai-detection-feature-has-divided-its-community)). **Inference:** a founder whose product writes marketing, publishing in a visibly human voice with data nobody else has, stands out on a platform that now labels everyone else. The growth path is the one the top B2B writers used: trade recommendations and guest posts with adjacent writers. Measure clicks and replies, not opens. beehiiv's average open rate of 41% is inflated by Apple Mail Privacy Protection ([beehiiv](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/the-state-of-newsletters-2026)).

**YouTube earns its place through AI citations and buyer research, not B2B entertainment.** YouTube leads AI citations on Perplexity (31.2%) and Google AI Overviews (21.1%) ([Contently on Ahrefs Brand Radar](https://contently.com/2026/04/29/top-sources-llms-cite/)), and YouTube brand mentions correlate about 0.737 with AI Overview visibility ([BrandMentions on Ahrefs](https://brandmentions.com/blog/brand-mentions-ai-visibility/)). About 60% of B2B buyers who used YouTube in research watched product tutorials ([Google/NRG](https://www.nrgmr.com/resources/Google%20B2B%20Buyer%20Journey_October_2025.pdf)), which makes the 16 Slate Academy videos a YouTube asset first. The caution: 50% of B2B marketers publish on YouTube but only 34% of professionals consume there ([Blazehive citing Ascend2/TopRank](https://www.blazehive.io/statistics/b2b-content-marketing-statistics/)). Generic B2B YouTube fails. **Recommendation:** retire "Founders for Founders" and its 20-episode target. Replace it with a six-episode video season of conversations with CMOs and founders whose brands moved in the Slate Index or whose results TripleDart delivered. Each guest becomes a distribution partner, and each episode produces clips, a Substack post and a transcript AI engines can index. "Founders" is too broad an audience for an agency and product that sell to B2B SaaS marketing leaders.

**AI-answer presence is a channel, and you don't own most of it.** In one study of 167,551 AI citations, **85.7% pointed to sites the brand doesn't own** ([arXiv 2606.25787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25787)). Branded web mentions correlate about 0.664 with AI Overview visibility, against 0.218 for backlinks ([BrandMentions](https://brandmentions.com/blog/brand-mentions-ai-visibility/)). For B2B software queries specifically, independent content and vendor comparison pages made up 79% of citations, and Reddit appeared in under 1% ([Omnibound](https://www.omnibound.ai/blog/reddit-data-b2b-ai-search)). Another vendor says Reddit appears in 34% of ChatGPT responses on B2B software ([Octolens](https://octolens.com/reddit-b2b-saas-study)), so treat Reddit as a hedge. G2 now runs a formal AEO category ([G2](https://www.g2.com/categories/answer-engine-optimization-aeo)), and review sites are the number-two shortlist influence at 43% ([G2 Learn](https://learn.g2.com/ai-search-surging-for-b2b-buyers)). **Recommendation:** Slate needs a steady flow of G2 reviews in that category. TripleDart needs a place on the third-party "best B2B SaaS agency" lists, and Slate on the "best AEO tools" lists. Both need honest comparison pages. Medium appears among Gemini and AI Overviews sources ([Peec AI](https://peec.ai/blog/top-domains-cited-by-ai-search-analysis-based-on-30m-sources)), but it offers no email ownership and no B2B network ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/11/mediums-ceo-explains-what-it-took-to-stop-losing-2-6m-monthly)), so it doesn't deserve more than a cross-post.

**X and Threads are not where B2B SaaS marketers are.** X's mobile daily users fell 7% to 123.7M, Threads grew 21.3% to 147M on consumer use, and Bluesky fell 25.6% ([TechCrunch via search summary](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3151942); [Slashdot](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/26/08/16/0326249/)). **Inference:** X matters only if Shiyam needs AI-builder and investor attention for Slate. If so, cross-post the data drops and spend no more than 15 minutes a day there.

## 4. The un-copyable content system: five franchises built on what only TripleDart and Slate know

**The test every piece must pass is one fact, number, name or artifact that no other company has.** AI can't produce numbers it has never seen. Buyers fact-check AI answers (94% do at least sometimes, per [MarketScale on TrustRadius](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/software-and-technology/94-of-b2b-buyers-fact-check-ai-research-and-peer-reviews-still-close-the-deal)), and the content they check against is first-hand data. Trust is concentrating in close, named circles: "my CEO" is trusted by 66% in Edelman's 2026 barometer ([Edelman](https://www.edelman.com/news-awards/2026-edelman-trust-barometer-society-slides-into-insularity)). Ben Thompson argues that provenance makes human work *more* valuable as AI output proliferates ([Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2026/ai-and-the-human-condition/), paraphrased from a summary). **Inference:** TripleDart's moats are its delivery data from 250+ clients and 500+ campaigns, a real operator team, a product that runs the work, and the Slate Index. Content that doesn't use one of them is commodity. Slate can and should do the production work (drafts, charts, cutdowns, distribution), with the facts and the final voice coming from people. That is also Slate's best pitch: AI that executes with *your* data and *your* voice. LinkedIn itself moved this way when it replaced its "enhance your post" rewriter with a proofreading-only tool ([Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/how-to-use-ai-on-linkedin-while-sounding-human/)).

| Franchise | Owner | Cadence | Built on | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **State of AI Search in B2B SaaS** (annual report + quarterly benchmark drops) | Shiyam + Akilan | Annual flagship; quarterly updates | TripleDart client data, the AI citation study, Slate Index | Substack launch issue, gated PDF, 4–6 carousels, dinner content, Thought Leader Ads |
| **Slate Index Monthly Movers** | Slate team, Akilan as named researcher | Monthly | 1,500 buyer questions across 50 AI companies | Index email list, LinkedIn chart posts, press/newsletter pitches, embeddable badges |
| **Inside-out teardowns** | Shiyam + delivery leads | Biweekly | Real TripleDart campaigns, with client permission, including failures | 10-slide carousel on LinkedIn; full version on Substack |
| **The services-as-software build log** | Shiyam | Monthly | TripleDart's own operating numbers | Substack and LinkedIn |
| **Live proof** | Slate team + Shiyam | Monthly | Slate running real work on camera | YouTube, LinkedIn clips, dinners |

**Make the year-end report the launch of everything else.** It is due in roughly six weeks. Combine TripleDart's client delivery data, Akilan's 2026 AI citation study and the Slate Index into one dated, quotable report, and use it to launch Shiyam's Substack. Each chart becomes a carousel, the best performers become Thought Leader Ads, and the findings become the agenda for the first two dinners. Gong is the model. Gong Labs turned customer call data into recurring research that it kept repurposing for months ([SalesFlint](https://salesflint.com/resources/brand-anatomy/gong-research-engine/)). Publish quarterly updates as well as the annual report, because AI answers are unstable. Only about 30% of brands stay visible from one AI answer to the next ([Digital Bloom citing AirOps](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/2025-ai-citation-llm-visibility-report/)), so one annual spike decays.

**Don't fight Profound on scale. Make the Slate Index the most quoted index, not the biggest.** Profound launched its index on stage at its own Zero Click conference, covering 1.5B+ prompts across 50+ industries ([Profound newsroom](https://www.tryprofound.com/newsroom/profound-launches-the-profound-index-at-zero-click-new-york-creating-the-definitive-benchmark-for-ai-search-visibility)). It reports 1,000+ marketing leaders at its 2026 SF and NYC events, plus a world tour ([TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/news/private-companies/profound-expands-zero-click-ai-marketing-summit-with-2026-global-tour)), and it has raised at least $96M (see section 8 on conflicting totals) ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/24/exclusive-as-ai-threatens-search-profound-raises-96-million-to-help-brands-stay-visible/)). Slate's 1,500 buyer questions can't win on volume. It can win on being specific to B2B SaaS buyer questions and on coming with a "what to do about it" section that only an operator team can write. **Recommendation:** design every release to be quoted. Publish a monthly top-movers list, one-sentence stats, per-category rankings and an embeddable badge companies want to show. Make Akilan the named, visible researcher behind it, the role Josh Blyskal plays at Profound ([Profound events](https://www.tryprofound.com/resources/events)). A named analyst is a trust signal, and an anonymous "Slate team" is not.

**Change teardowns from outside-in to inside-out.** "Growth Playbook II: Ramp" in 21 sections is impressive work, but anyone with Claude and a browser can now produce an outside-in teardown of a public company. Nobody else can publish what TripleDart actually ran for a named client: the numbers, what failed, what changed, and how long it took. **Recommendation:** shift at least half the teardowns to real engagements, with client permission, and end each one with "what Slate now runs automatically." That ending turns each teardown into product proof, which fits the land-and-expand model where TripleDart services carry Slate into accounts.

**Publish the build log, because skeptics will ask whether "services as software" is just an agency with better tools.** Investors and peers already ask whether AI-native services firms are "simply modern services firms with better tooling" ([eMerge](https://emcap.substack.com/p/the-ai-roll-up-trap)). Emergence describes the category as selling outcomes, with margins that improve as models improve ([Emergence](https://www.emcap.com/thoughts/the-death-of-deloitte-ai-enabled-services-are-opening-a-whole-new-market)). **Recommendation:** Shiyam publishes TripleDart's own numbers every month: the share of delivery tasks Slate runs, output per strategist, time to first result, and whatever outcome-based pricing experiments are running. Nobody can fake this, because a named founder is accountable for numbers that have to stay consistent over time. It also gives the repositioning substance. A borrowed label becomes a documented transformation. It also needs a proprietary name for TripleDart's *method*, the way Grow and Convert built a brand on "pain point SEO" and then turned the method into software (Traqer AI) ([Tenspeed](https://www.tenspeed.io/blog/b2b-growth-agency)). That is the same path TripleDart is on with Slate.

**Live proof is the cheapest stunt available.** Ramp's lesson is that one surprising, character-driven moment can become a series. Its livestream with Brian Baumgartner turned into a Super Bowl campaign ([Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2025/10/22/ramp-viral-livestream-brian-baumgartner); [LBB Online](https://lbbonline.com/news/Ramp-Super-Bowl-LX-Brian-Baumgartner)). **Recommendation:** run a monthly live AI-visibility audit of a volunteer B2B SaaS brand, with Slate doing the work on screen and Shiyam narrating the decisions. Or run "Slate versus a three-person marketing team for a week." AI-generated video can't fake an unedited session where the product either works or doesn't. Add a quarterly **predictions scorecard**, where Shiyam publishes dated calls about AI search and GTM and grades last quarter's. No study proves track records build trust (**inference**), but nobody can generate a track record on demand. Finally, use **customer voices**: client-co-authored posts and video case studies. Seventy-four percent of buyers use reviews ([Demand Gen Report on TrustRadius](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/news-brief/trustradius-ai-has-changed-how-buyers-research-but-not-what-they-trust/53853/)).

Everything else on the current list folds into these five or stops. The "thought leadership series" and "big bet" ideas survive only if they carry proprietary data. Slate Academy moves to YouTube as tutorials.

## 5. The amplification engine: credible people replace the pod

The engine has one rule: **every share has to come from someone who would plausibly say it anyway, to an audience that would plausibly buy.** Seven parts make it work.

**Shiyam's own commenting is the highest-return unpaid activity.** He should spend 20–30 minutes a day writing substantive, clearly human comments on posts by ICP buyers (B2B SaaS CMOs and founders) and adjacent creators. Comments surface in their networks, while automated ones get filtered ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-outlines-more-measures-to-combat-engagement-pods/812290/)). He should track which target accounts comment on his posts, as Koka Sexton suggests, because those comments are buying signals ([SignalScout](https://signalscout.kokasexton.com/newsletter/founder-led-growth-linkedin)).

**Five to eight operators should each own a topic lane and post their own work.** Employee posts reportedly earn 2.75x the impressions and 5x the engagement of company-page posts ([salesso citing Refine Labs](https://salesso.com/blog/linkedin-organic-reach-statistics/)). Akilan posts citation research, a delivery lead posts campaign results, a Slate PM posts what users actually do. **Recommendation:** turn #socialmediaupdates from "please engage" into a library. Each new post goes in with one line on who it helps. Teammates comment only when they have something to add, and the default action is to DM the post to one person outside the company it would help. That is dark social, the channel Refine Labs found software attribution misses almost entirely ([Refine Labs](https://www.refinelabs.com/blog/attribution-mirage)).

**Clients and partner agencies are the distribution Clay built and Profound doesn't have.** Clay turned GTM-engineering agencies into its channel: 90+ agencies building businesses on Clay by January 2025, and 60+ Clay Clubs worldwide ([Yespress](https://yespress.io/clay); [Clay](https://www.clay.com/series-c)). Slate for Agencies is the obvious counterpart. Give partner agencies vertical cuts of the Slate Index to publish under their own logo, a certification, a directory listing, and support to host local dinners. TripleDart becomes the flagship Slate agency, not a rival to the partners. **Inference, and the main risk:** partner agencies will hesitate if they think TripleDart will take their clients. The rules of engagement have to be explicit, possibly with a firmer brand separation between Slate and TripleDart. On the client side, TripleDart's 250+ clients are the source of co-authored case studies, podcast guests and Slate reviews on G2, which is the services-give-Slate-distribution model applied to brand.

**Pay creators to use Slate on camera, not to post about it.** Fifty-five percent of B2B marketers already use LinkedIn creators ([ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/linkedin-influencer-marketing-b2b-playbook/)), and HubSpot's creator program became the feeder for its media acquisitions ([Rockwater](https://chris-erwin-rockwater-newsletter.beehiiv.com/p/hubspot-x-starter-story-the-case-for-saas-media-m-a)). **Recommendation:** recruit 6–10 B2B SaaS marketing creators to run real AI-visibility audits with Slate or the Index, and buy amplification rights. Creators charge a 25–40% premium for those rights ([Clash.cc](https://clash.cc/insights/linkedin-thought-leader-ads-vs-b2b-influencer)). Run the results as Thought Leader Ads. The creators' content becomes third-party mentions, which is the input that correlates with AI visibility.

**Use Thought Leader Ads to amplify proven posts, and judge them on pipeline.** ZenABM's benchmarks show TLAs at a 2.68% CTR and $2.29 CPC, against 0.42% and $13.23 for single-image ads ([ZenABM](https://zenabm.com/blog/tla-benchmarks/)). The skeptical reading matters, though. LinkedIn counts reactions and comments as "clicks" for TLAs, and a paired benchmark found landing-page CTR at near parity, 0.449% versus 0.441% ([Understory](https://www.understoryagency.com/blog/linkedin-thought-leader-ads-vs-brand-ads)). **Recommendation:** boost only posts that already performed organically with the ICP, target named account lists, and judge the spend on pipeline and self-reported attribution. As arithmetic on the vendor CPM of $49.37, a $4,000 monthly test buys about 80,000 targeted impressions. That is enough to learn from in one quarter.

**Dinners turn attention into relationships, and only after that is a flagship worth it.** Curated dinners of 10–30 people cost $5K–$30K each and work best with 60–70% customers ([GrowthSpree](https://www.growthspreeofficial.com/blogs/build-b2b-saas-field-marketing-events-program-from-zero-playbook-2026)). One company reported about 40% conversion from attendee to opportunity (an anecdote: [Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/how-to-build-more-pipeline-in-2024?r=gr77p)). Forrester predicts buyers will double their human interactions to validate AI recommendations ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/predictions-2027-ai-takes-over-business-buying-but-humans-retain-control/)). **Recommendation:** hold the planned flagship event until dinners prove demand. Run an "AI search for B2B SaaS" dinner series, co-hosted with a non-competing brand the way Profound used Ramp's HQ ([Profound on X](https://x.com/tryprofound/status/1960756624433586386)), and tie each dinner to a data drop. A head-on conference against Zero Click's 1,000-plus attendees is a fight TripleDart doesn't need to have yet.

**Treat AI-answer presence as the end of the engine, not a separate project.** Every franchise, creator audit, review and partner post adds third-party mentions, and those are the strongest correlate of AI visibility. Slate should measure this on TripleDart and on itself, and publish the results. The scorecard is product proof.

A realistic weekly load for Shiyam is about 8–10 hours. That breaks down as one anchor piece (4–6 hours, with Akilan and an editor), three to four LinkedIn posts drawn from it (1–2 hours), daily commenting (about 2 hours a week) and one dinner a month. **If that is too much, move the anchor to every other week before cutting the commenting.**

## 6. The time-phased plan: what brand building looks like and what to do

### Next 3–6 months (November 2026 to April 2027): fix the signal, ship the flagship, start owning the audience

**What the landscape looks like.** LinkedIn keeps tightening against pods and AI filler ([CNN via Style Magazine](http://stylemagazine.com/news/2026/oct/19/even-linkedin-is-getting-tired-of-your-ai-written-posts/)), and it sells organic reach back through TLAs and BrandLink. ChatGPT runs a self-serve ad platform, open since May 2026 ([Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/chatgpt-ads-2026-guide)). The share of buyers who start research in AI went from 29% to 51% in about a year ([G2](https://learn.g2.com/g2-2026-ai-search-insight-report)). **Inference (extrapolation):** that share passes 60% by mid-2027.

**What to do.** In the first two weeks, pull Shiyam's LinkedIn analytics (section 8), pick the lane, and rewrite the headlines and About sections for Shiyam and the 5–8 operators. Retire the first-hour requests the same week. The lane I'd recommend is "how B2B SaaS companies get found and chosen when buyers start in AI, shown with data from the work TripleDart and Slate run." AI-search visibility (Slate) and the services-as-software operating model (TripleDart) are the two allowed sub-themes, and everything else comes off his feed. In December and January, ship the year-end report as the State of AI Search in B2B SaaS, launch the Substack with it, run its carousels as TLAs at about $3–5K a month, and host two dinners around its findings. From January, publish Index Monthly Movers and the biweekly inside-out teardowns. Cut SEO output to 2–3 pages a week of comparison and bottom-funnel content with proprietary facts. Make "How did you hear about us?" a required open-text field on every demo form for both brands. Fix a prompt set for AI visibility and take a baseline. Start a G2 review drive for Slate in the AEO category, and pitch TripleDart and Slate to the third-party "best of" lists. Record the first three episodes of the six-episode video season.

**What to measure.** The share of Shiyam's post viewers in ICP titles and companies (LinkedIn demographics), named target-account engagers per month, Substack subscribers and the share from Recommendations, replies and clicks, self-reported attribution mentions, the AI visibility baseline, and third-party mentions of the report and the Index.

**Signposts that change the plan.** After 90 days on one lane, impressions may stay flat while ICP viewer share and target-account engagers rise. That means it's working, so keep going. If ICP metrics fall too, the problem is the content, not the topic, so revisit the franchises before abandoning the lane. If after six months fewer than a quarter of new Substack subscribers come from Recommendations, the network effect isn't arriving. That threshold is a judgment call: Lenny and Kyle got half or more ([Growth in Reverse](https://growthinreverse.substack.com/p/the-5-biggest-takeaways-from-lennys); [Thormeier](https://blog.thormeier.co/p/how-to-build-and-monetize-a-newsletter)). In that case, move the anchor to YouTube plus the company list. Cut TLAs if two quarters produce no attributable pipeline.

### One year (by late 2027): build the ecosystem and the machine-readable brand

**What the landscape looks like.** Forrester predicts AI agents will influence **75% of B2B purchase decisions in 2027**, and that buyers will double human validation through references, partners and provider teams ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/press-newsroom/forresters-2027-b2b-go-to-market-predictions-ai-agents-will-influence-75-of-b2b-purchase-decisions/)). Agent-led buying runs mostly through procurement suites, for routine purchases ([BlueBolt](https://www.blueboltsolutions.com/agentic-commerce-b2b-manufacturing-2026/)). **Inference:** paid AI placements become a standard budget line next to Google Ads, and vendors that publish machine-readable trust data (pricing, security, integrations) are easier for agents to shortlist. No primary evidence yet shows *how* buying agents build shortlists.

**What to do.** Turn Slate for Agencies into an ecosystem with certification, a directory, partner-hosted dinners and co-branded vertical Index cuts. Launch the creator program. Hold a flagship event only if dinners are producing waitlists, co-host it, and use it to launch the 2027 report on stage, the way Profound launched its index. Give Slate a machine-readable trust layer: public pricing, documentation, a security and trust page, an integration list, and an API or MCP endpoint if customers ask for one. Treat the MCP endpoint as a bet. Test ChatGPT ads for Slate as a capture channel, not as the strategy. Publish TripleDart's and Slate's brand scorecard openly as dogfooding. Name TripleDart's method and keep it.

**What to measure.** AI visibility share across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude and AI Overviews over many runs, the share of pipeline that self-reports a brand channel, third-party citations of the Index and benchmarks, active partner agencies and the pipeline they source, and event-influenced pipeline.

**Signposts.** If AI answers turn pay-to-play (rising ad load, "Sponsored Agents" blending with answers, per [Prowlo](https://prowlo.com/blog/chatgpt-ads-ai-agents)), move budget toward the owned audience and human channels. If model vendors let agents query vendor APIs during shortlisting, invest more in structured data. If partner agencies won't join because of TripleDart, separate the brands. If Profound moves further into execution or agency channels, sharpen what makes Slate different. Check this quarterly, because the competitive set moves that fast.

### Three years (2029): brand becomes a reputation graph

**What the landscape looks like (low confidence on size, reasonable confidence on direction).** Most B2B discovery and first-pass evaluation runs through AI, and website traffic stops being the main top-of-funnel metric. Brand becomes how models *and* peers describe you, built from reviews, community discussion, creator and analyst coverage and customer outcomes. Content farms fade, and owned audiences plus human-led media become the premium trust layer. Treat precise forecasts with caution. Gartner's 2024 call for a 25% drop in search volume by 2026 did not show up in the aggregate data ([BrandGEO](https://brandgeo.co/blog/gartner-25-percent-search-drop-what-to-model)), even though the direction was right.

**What to do.** By 2029 Shiyam should be the recognised voice for one idea, and the owned audience should be TripleDart's and Slate's main distribution. The Index and benchmarks should be the reference others cite on AI search in B2B SaaS, a Gong Labs for the category. The partner ecosystem should source a meaningful share of Slate pipeline. Once cash allows, consider HubSpot's path of partnering with creators and then buying them ([Rockwater](https://chris-erwin-rockwater-newsletter.beehiiv.com/p/hubspot-x-starter-story-the-case-for-saas-media-m-a)): sponsor, co-own or acquire a niche B2B SaaS GTM newsletter or podcast. Settle the brand architecture by then. If Slate grows faster, Slate leads and TripleDart becomes its services arm. If it doesn't, TripleDart leads as "the agency that runs on its own agent."

**What to measure.** Owned-audience reach and engagement, the share of pipeline from owned, community and partner channels, AI share of voice on category prompts against Profound and others, and citations of the Index.

**Signposts.** Model providers giving licensed or verified sources more weight would raise the value of review platforms and publisher partnerships. A trust backlash against AI recommendations after an ad or manipulation scandal would push value toward human channels. Agent-to-agent procurement becoming standard would make structured trust data mandatory.

## 7. How to measure brand now: replace followers with a six-line scorecard

Software attribution misses most of what brand does. In Refine Labs' data, HubSpot credited web search with 78% of conversions, while buyers named it in only 12% of self-reports ([Refine Labs](https://www.refinelabs.com/blog/attribution-mirage)). Refine sells this method and recall bias is real ([Recast](https://getrecast.com/hdyhau/)), so combine self-reports with the other signals. AI rank is noise. SparkToro found less than a 1-in-100 chance of getting the same brand list twice from ChatGPT or Google AI ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-recommendations-change-with-nearly-every-query-sparktoro/566242/)).

| Metric | Source | Cadence | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Self-reported attribution naming Shiyam, LinkedIn, Substack, the Index, an event, a peer or a partner | Required open-text field on demo forms (TripleDart and Slate) | Monthly | The only direct read on dark social |
| ICP reach: share of post viewers in target titles and companies; named target-account engagers | LinkedIn analytics plus a CRM match | Monthly | Replaces follower counts and impressions |
| Owned audience: Substack and Index-list subscribers, Recommendation share, replies, clicks | Substack, HubSpot/beehiiv | Monthly | Distribution that survives algorithm changes |
| Branded search impressions for "TripleDart," "Slate HQ," "Slate Index" | Google Search Console | Monthly | Correlates about 0.33–0.39 with AI visibility ([BrandMentions](https://brandmentions.com/blog/brand-mentions-ai-visibility/)) |
| AI visibility share on a fixed prompt set, across engines, over many runs | Slate (dogfooded) | Weekly sampling, monthly review | How shortlists now form; publishable proof |
| Third-party mentions and citations of the Index and benchmarks; G2 reviews; listicle inclusions | Mention tracking, G2 | Monthly | The input most correlated with AI visibility |
| Event-influenced pipeline | CRM (open pipeline at attending accounts) | Per event and quarterly | Attendance alone measures activity, not impact |

Stop reporting follower counts as a goal, raw impressions, TLA "CTR," email open rates and single-run AI rankings. Each looks like progress without telling you whether the right buyers remember you.

## 8. Caveats and what to verify before acting

**The most important missing input is Shiyam's own data.** This report did not look at his LinkedIn analytics or TripleDart's posts. The topic-diffusion and pod explanations fit the platform's mechanics, but they haven't been measured. Before the plan locks, pull 24 months of impressions by post topic, the share of engagement coming from TripleDart employees, and viewer demographics (titles, companies, regions). A drop well beyond the 35–60% market range, a heavy share of employee engagement or viewers who don't match the ICP would confirm the self-inflicted causes. A drop near the median would mean the market explains most of it.

**Most figures are vendor data, often read from summaries.** Fetches of linkedin.com, AuthoredUp, Socialinsider, Social Media Today, Creator Science, ppc.land, TechCrunch, Stratechery, beehiiv, Clay, Forrester and Profound were blocked in research, so many numbers come from search-result summaries. Van der Blom's figures vary across summaries: a drop of 50% or 60%, and a sample of 1.3M or 1.8M posts. The claim that "360Brew ranks your posts" is unconfirmed marketer lore. The sequential-ranker account rests on the arXiv paper. Originality.ai sells the detector it used. The Raptive and Edelman–LinkedIn figures are secondhand. G2 has a commercial interest in framing AI buying as review-dependent. TLA benchmarks conflict. Reddit's share of AI citations is volatile and contested for B2B queries. Clay's and Profound's numbers are company-reported. **Profound's funding is reported inconsistently:** $96M at a $1B valuation ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/24/exclusive-as-ai-threatens-search-profound-raises-96-million-to-help-brands-stay-visible/)), $180M at $1.8B ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/nicole-gull-mcelroy/how-profound-became-your-companys-chatgpt-and-claude-whisperer/91391093)), and over $335M total after a September 2026 Series D ([Cairrot](https://cairrot.com/blog/which-of-the-big-three-aeo-tools-is-best-profound-vs-peec-ai-vs-scrunch-ai-2026/)). Profound's current product scope should be re-checked, because AEO vendors change positioning every quarter. Forecasts (Forrester's 75%, the extrapolation to 60% starting in AI) indicate direction, not size. No rigorous study isolates original data, track records or live formats as causes of trust. Those recommendations rest on converging indirect evidence and on what made Gong, Clay and Profound work. The time budgets, spend levels and thresholds in this report are judgment, meant to be tuned against the first 90 days of data.

## Conclusion

The habit to drop is treating brand as a volume problem. TripleDart's instinct (more posts, more series, more blogs, more first-hour engagement) was rational when the feed rewarded output and network activity. The feed now rewards topical clarity, attention and accountable specifics, and buyers' AI assistants reward whatever other people repeat about you. Both favour a company that publishes less, in one lane, with numbers only it has, and gets other credible people to carry it.

The less obvious point is that Slate's best marketing is TripleDart's brand discipline. A company selling an AI marketing agent proves the product by being visibly the most human, most specific voice in its category, and by showing the agent doing the production work underneath. If Shiyam's feed, Substack and Index look like that within six months, the content works as the pitch. If they look like everyone else's AI output, the content undercuts the product.

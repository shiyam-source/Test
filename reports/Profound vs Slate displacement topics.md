# Profound Moves Upmarket, Opening Slate's Lane

Profound is no longer the company Slate's comparison pages describe. In the fourteen months to November 2026 it went from an AI visibility tracker to an "AI Marketer" with agents, an always-on orchestrator (Aim), an ads product and a services channel. Around its September 2026 Series D it also reportedly removed the $99 and $399 self-serve plans for brands, so a brand now gets a 7-day trial and then a sales-led Enterprise quote. Two consequences follow for Slate. First, the line Slate leads with today ("Profound observes, Slate executes") is out of date, and dozens of vendor listicles already repeat it. Second, a more specific and more defensible lane has opened. It covers statistical honesty about what a prompt quota can detect, the stale facts that AI engines now repeat about every vendor (Slate included), who actually does the execution work (Profound is building a GSI and agency channel; Slate ships with TripleDart operators), and procurement questions for buyers whose only remaining path to Profound is an annual Enterprise contract. The fifteen topics below are built on those gaps. They are sequenced around one tentpole asset, a "Coin-Flip Report" showing how many AI answers a visibility number needs before it means anything. Profound is still stronger on capital, enterprise logos, panel-based prompt data, engine breadth and support, and the content has to say so. Every price and quote here came from search snippets, because tryprofound.com, slatehq.com, G2 and Reddit were blocked to the researchers. The verification list at the end is a precondition for publishing.

## Profound has traded the dashboard and the middle market for agents and channels

**The messaging arc shows the pivot.** Profound raised about **$335M in roughly 27 months**. Its self-description changed with each round: "$20M to pioneer Answer Engine Optimization" (Series A, June 2025), "$35M to connect brands with one new customer: Superintelligence" (Series B, August 2025), "to Lead a New Category of Marketing" (Series C, $96M at $1B, February 2026), and "to Build the AI Platform For Marketing Teams" (Series D, **$180M at $1.8B, September 15 2026**) ([Profound blog](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/series-a); [Profound blog](https://tryprofound.com/blog/series-b); [Profound newsroom](https://www.tryprofound.com/newsroom/profound-raises-series-c-at-1b-valuation-to-lead-a-new-category-of-marketing); [Yahoo Finance/press release](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/profound-raises-180m-series-d-130000726.html)). The product followed. Agents launched formally in February 2026 ([Profound changelog](https://product.tryprofound.com/changelog/announcing-our-series-c)), an Agent Template Marketplace with "50+ production ready agents" in June ([Profound blog](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/introducing-the-profound-agent-template-marketplace)), Aim, an "always-on background agent" that "coordinates execution," on July 2 ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/02/3321345/0/en/profound-launches-aim-to-transform-ai-search-data-into-marketing-execution.html)), and Ads Studio on July 7 ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/media-advertising/articles/profound-launches-ads-studio-helping-130000912.html)). In October came AEO Content Score, Dynamic Bot Rendering, Projects ("a shared workspace for your team and your AI Marketer") and a rebuilt Agents product: "Building an Agent in Profound is now a conversation. Describe the job to your AI Marketer, give it the tools it needs, and it figures out the rest" ([Profound changelog](https://product.tryprofound.com/changelog)). Search Engine Watch reports the AI Marketer "finds earned media opportunities, proactively refreshes stale content… and even launches new ads" ([Search Engine Watch](https://searchenginewatch.com/profound-launches-context-manager-for-ai-marketer/)). That makes **four agent paradigms in about eight months**. It also means Profound now markets almost the same "coworker that executes" story Slate does.

**The self-serve middle was quietly removed in September.** Profound's brand pricing went through three stages. A **$499/month "Lite" plan** launched with the Series A in June 2025 ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/profound-raises-20m-as-brands-race-from-blue-links-to-ai-answers-302486211.html)). It was followed by **$99 Starter (50 prompts, ChatGPT only) and $399 Growth (100 prompts, three engines), billed annually only** ([Get-Ryze](https://www.get-ryze.ai/blog/profound-pricing-2026)). Then, per multiple trackers, both brand plans were removed: "reported gone by September 17 and confirmed gone on September 26 and 28," which leaves "a short free trial and a custom-priced Enterprise plan you reach through a sales demo" ([GeoToolbox](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/profound-pricing); [CitePrism](https://citeprism.com/blog/profound-pricing); [eClincher](https://www.eclincher.com/articles/profound-alternatives-8-ai-visibility-tools-after-profounds-pricing-change)). The removal happened the same week as the Series D. Agencies kept a self-serve path: **"Agency Growth" at $99/month base plus $399 per client workspace** (100 prompts, three engines, run daily) ([Trakkr](https://trakkr.ai/reviews/profound-review/pricing); [Tools8020](https://tools8020.com/tools/profound/pricing/)). Profound has not published an Enterprise price. Third parties estimate "$2,000 to $5,000+ per month" ([GeoToolbox](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/profound-pricing)), and a second-hand Vendr figure puts the **median contract at about $34,500/year** ([Reoptimize](https://reoptimize.ai/profound-pricing)). Claude, Gemini and Copilot tracking, SSO, API, all-time history and exports are reported as Enterprise-only ([MartechVerdict](https://martechverdict.com/reviews/profound-pricing/); [Trakkr integrations](https://trakkr.ai/reviews/profound-review/integrations)). For a mid-market brand, nothing is published between a 7-day trial and a roughly $2,000-plus monthly contract.

**Execution at the top end is being routed through services partners.** A June 2026 job post for a VP Strategic Partnerships asks the hire to "Build and scale a Global Systems Integrator (GSI) program, establishing and deepening relationships with the likes of Accenture, Deloitte, Bain, Cap Gemini etc." ([Ashby job post](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/Profound/816c2ab6-7398-4cd8-b1b5-695d4673b476)). A Certified Agency Partner program (iPullRank, Greenpark, Avenue Z) and an invite-only Partner Kickoff in New York on October 1 2026 sit alongside it ([Avenue Z release](https://finance.yahoo.com/media-advertising/articles/avenue-z-named-profound-agency-140000066.html); [Profound events](https://www.tryprofound.com/events/partner-kickoff-2026)). Profound's CEO says marketing teams can "take on" more work with "purpose built AI" ([press release](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/profound-raises-180m-series-d-130000726.html)), while the go-to-market pays agencies and integrators to do that work. Reviewers note the product works best with "a dedicated AEO or SEO lead driving it" ([Geoptie](https://geoptie.com/blog/profound-review)).

**Growth is reported in logos, not revenue.** Customer claims went from "more than 700 enterprises" in February to "1,000 enterprise brands including one third of the Fortune 100" in September ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/profound-96-million-series-c-raised-at-1-billion-valuation-for-ai-native-marketing-platform/); [everything-pr](https://everything-pr.com/profound-valuation-series-d)). Profound's own blog says "2,500+ brands" ([CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/digital-experience/profound-nabs-180m-to-scale-ai-marketing-platform/)). TechCrunch reports revenue up **3x in six months**, per the company ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/15/aeo-startup-profound-hits-unicorn-valuation-raises-180m-series-d-7-months-after-last-round/)). No ARR has been disclosed, and one trade outlet called the round "attribution froth" ([ic.work](https://www.ic.work/article/aeo-startup-profound-raises-180m-series-d-reaches-unicorn-va)). Slate should not attack the valuation, since doing so looks petty. The useful point is narrower: "one third of the Fortune 100" tells a 200-person SaaS company little about fit for its own situation.

**The data moat has an audit problem.** Profound's prompt data comes from double-opt-in consumer panels with "no synthetic prompts" ([Trakkr data accuracy](https://trakkr.ai/reviews/profound-review/data-accuracy)). The volumes are "probabilistic modeling" ([Nick Lafferty](https://nicklafferty.com/blog/prompt-volume-aeo-explained/)), and the dataset size is given variously as 130M, 400M and "1.5B+" conversations ([Trakkr](https://trakkr.ai/reviews/profound-review/data-accuracy); [Profound blog](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/introducing-prompt-research-reports-in-profound)). In one practitioner spot-check, "ai email agents" showed **9,800 monthly prompts in Profound, 40 searches in Ahrefs and 45 GSC impressions**. The same author says Profound's Head of Data recommends sanity-checking against GSC ([Raf Rivera, LinkedIn](https://ph.linkedin.com/in/rafrivera)). This is one anecdote and should be treated as one.

**Profound now grades its own work.** As of October 2026, one Profound workspace scores content (AEO Content Score), writes it (Agents/AI Marketer) and measures whether it worked (Answer Engine Insights) ([Profound changelog](https://product.tryprofound.com/changelog)). That is the deepest structural tension in its pitch. It applies equally to Slate, which is why the topic below proposes a standard Slate holds itself to.

**Reviews are clean, but the complaints are consistent.** G2 shows about **4.5 stars from roughly 1,040–1,130 reviews with essentially no 1–2 star ratings**, plus the Leader badge in G2's first AEO category ([G2 seller page](https://www.g2.com/sellers/profound-d24227c1-f7ad-47c0-a57d-e2432d418f1c); [G2 reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/profound/reviews)). That pattern suggests solicited reviews, which makes the dislikes that show up inside 4–5 star reviews more credible: "rather expensive in comparison to other tools" ([Scalenut](https://www.scalenut.com/blogs/profound-ai-reviews)), "limited prompt availability for the price" ([ContentMonk](https://www.contentmonk.io/aeo-geo-tools/profound-review)), "there is a lot of data to go through, so it can feel a bit overwhelming at first" ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/profound/reviews?page=10)), "dashboards take weeks to learn, and we still don't use most of them" ([AI Growth Agent](https://aigrowthagent.co/articles/profound-ai-review/)), and "has become unstable, slow and tedious lately" ([GetMint](https://getmint.ai/resources/profound-review)). One reviewer summary reads: "users know where they're missing but aren't sure what to do next" ([Sightivo](https://sightivo.com/blog/profound-review)). Reddit is close to silent on Profound. A vendor scrape found no threads ([Mentionova](https://mentionova.com/blog/profound-reviews-reddit)), and the practitioner consensus summarized there rates Profound "highest on depth and lowest on simplicity" ([Linkeddit](https://linkeddit.com/blog/ai-visibility-tools-reddit-verdict)). The honest-comparison conversation in communities is open for Slate to join.

## Profound is stronger on depth, capital and coverage, and Slate's own pages are stale

Slate's content will only be believed if it concedes Profound's real advantages. Profound has more capital than the rest of the category combined (**$335M of about $604M raised by AI-search-optimization startups**) ([ValueAdd VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/ai-search-optimization-startups-2026-geo-aeo-funding-investors)). Its named customers include Comcast, Walmart, Estée Lauder, RBC, Zoom, MongoDB and Figma ([CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/digital-experience/profound-nabs-180m-to-scale-ai-marketing-platform/)). Enterprise customers get coverage of up to 9–11 engines ([SE Visible](https://visible.seranking.com/blog/profound-review/)). G2 reviewers praise front-end querying: it "makes the data far cleaner and more actionable than platforms relying on API pulls" ([Writesonic](https://writesonic.com/blog/profound-review)). That is a methodological point in Profound's favor, given Surfer's finding that API answers lacked sources about 25% of the time ([Surfer SEO](https://surferseo.com/blog/llm-scraped-ai-answers-vs-api-results)). Reviewers also credit quick onboarding, weekly rep sessions and fast shipping ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/profound/reviews)). And Profound has shipped agentic execution, an ads product, an Enterprise MCP server and a Slack-ingesting Context Manager ([Profound blog](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/the-profound-mcp-evolution-part-1); [Search Engine Watch](https://searchenginewatch.com/profound-launches-context-manager-for-ai-marketer/)). Any Slate claim that Profound "only monitors," "has no agents," "can't do ads," "has no data moat" or "ignores agencies" is false as of November 2026. On price per unit, Slate is not cheaper either. Using the reported figures, Profound's agency workspace works out to about **$0.044 per sampled answer** (9,000 answers for $399) and Slate Growth to about **$0.050** (10,000 answers for $499). Slate's real edges are unlimited prompts, the bundled execution credits, a fourth engine (Google AI Mode) and published self-serve pricing ([Slate blog](https://slatehq.com/blog/search-atlas-alternatives-compared); [Slate vs Profound](https://slatehq.com/slate-vs-profound)).

Slate also has its own stale claims, and they should be fixed before any displacement campaign goes out. The worst would be a "we audited the alternatives pages" study that catches Slate's own errors.

### 2. Tentpole: "The Coin-Flip Report: how many AI answers it takes before a visibility number means anything"

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Non-obvious angle | SparkToro showed AI answers are random. Nobody has turned that into vendor quota terms. This study maps each common quota design (Profound agency workspace: 100 prompts × 3 engines × daily; Profound trial: 50 × 3 × 7 days; Slate Growth: a 10,000-answer pool) to the smallest change it can reliably detect. |
| Core evidence and quotes | Under a 1-in-100 chance of the same brand list on a repeat run, and under 1-in-1,000 for the same order ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-recommendations-change-with-nearly-every-query-sparktoro/566242/)). Fishkin: "any tool that gives a 'ranking position in AI' is full of baloney" (secondary quote, [Dubado](https://dubadosolutions.com/rand-fishkin-proved-ai-recommendations-are-inconsistent-heres-why-and-how-to-fix-it/)). A 1% mention rate over 100 runs has a 95% CI of about 0.2–5.5% ([ThriveStack](https://www.thrivestack.ai/research/ai-visibility-tracking-non-determinism)). **Quota math (our Wilson-interval arithmetic):** one daily run gives about 30 samples per prompt-engine per month. At a 30% appearance rate, the interval is **16.7%–47.9%**, and a month-over-month change has to exceed about **23 points** to count as real. At 100 runs that falls to about 13 points. Kevin Indig: "the thing they're measuring isn't stable" ([Growth Memo](https://substack.com/@kevinindig/note/c-302753921)). |
| Why it's different | It turns category skepticism into a buyer-usable spec. It is also the first study to publish confidence intervals by plan design. |
| Slate proof point | Unlimited prompts plus a shared answer pool let a team move sampling depth onto the prompts it is actively fixing (for example 100 prompts × 4 engines × 25 runs = 10,000 answers). Show Slate reporting intervals; if the product doesn't yet, ship that first. |
| Format | Data study (interactive page plus PDF), open methodology, downloadable dataset, and a webinar. |
| Target reader | Heads of SEO/content and VPs Marketing who report AI visibility to leadership. |
| Risks / claims to avoid | The finding applies to Slate as well, so say that on page one. Disclose the collection method (API vs UI, logged-out, region) and any conflict of interest, as SparkToro/Gumshoe did. Have an outside statistician review the work. Don't say Profound's data is "wrong". Say that per-prompt daily sampling can't resolve small monthly changes. |

### 4. "Seven days, fifty prompts: what you can learn about your AI visibility before Profound's demo wall"

| Target reader | Mid-market evaluators in an active buying cycle. |
| Risks / claims to avoid | Highest boomerang risk on the list. Sources disagree on Profound's trial terms (10 prompts ChatGPT-only, or no trial at all). Don't publish until both trials are verified. |

## Conclusion

The competitive question has changed. Profound is no longer a dashboard Slate can out-execute in a headline. It is a well-funded execution platform that has chosen enterprise contracts and services channels over the self-serve middle. Slate's best displacement content therefore isn't louder comparison. It is content that sets standards: what a quota can statistically detect, how stale facts spread through AI answers, who is accountable for the work, and how a buyer can audit any vendor, Slate included. Each of those turns a Profound strength (scale, enterprise focus, a vertically integrated "AI Marketer") into a question its sales-led, quote-only model answers slowly, while Slate can answer it publicly and in writing.

The biggest risk is self-inflicted. Slate's own pages carry a discontinued $99 Profound price, an execution-vs-monitoring line Profound has made obsolete, inconsistent tier names, and possibly a trial far smaller than Profound's. Fixing those in week 1, and saying openly that it did, is both the prerequisite for the campaign and its first piece of evidence.

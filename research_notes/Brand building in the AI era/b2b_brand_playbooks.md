## 1. Case studies: B2B brands that built outsized brand in 2024–2026, and how

### Takeaway
The clearest documented wins share one pattern. The company named something (a role, a category, an index or an event) and then built an ecosystem of humans who could profit from that name: agencies, creators, community clubs and attendees. Clay ("GTM engineer" plus agencies and clubs) and Profound (the Zero Click event series plus the Profound Index) are the models closest to Slate and TripleDart. Ramp shows that a single well-timed B2B stunt can outperform paid TV. Granola shows that brand and design can be credited by investors as a differentiator. Hard, audited numbers are rare. Most figures are company-reported.

### Inferences
- **Clay is the best template for TripleDart plus Slate.** Clay turned a services profession (GTM engineering agencies) into its distribution channel. TripleDart already *is* a GTM engineering agency, and Slate has "Slate for Agencies." The analog is to make Slate the platform that lets other agencies build AI-search or marketing-execution businesses, with certification, a directory and revenue stories. TripleDart then becomes the flagship "Slate agency," not a competitor to the partner network.
- **Profound's playbook is replicable at lower cost.** It combines (a) founder-thesis podcasts, (b) a branded event series, often co-hosted at another brand's office (Ramp HQ), and (c) an index launched *on stage*. Slate Index (slateindex.ai) is the obvious counterpart. The difference has to be the angle (for example, B2B SaaS-specific, agency-facing, or execution-oriented rather than monitoring-only), not head-to-head spend.
- Ramp's lesson is not "buy a Super Bowl ad." It is that one surprising, character-driven stunt can be serialized. At Slate's scale, the equivalent is a cheap, live, provocative demonstration, such as "Slate vs. a human marketing team for a week," livestreamed.
- HubSpot's "partner with creators, then acquire" model is a scaled version of what a $6M agency can do in miniature. That could mean sponsoring or co-owning a niche newsletter or podcast in the B2B SaaS GTM space.

## 2. Category creation and naming ("GTM engineering," "AEO") as a brand lever

### Takeaway
Owning a term works when the company also creates economic incentives for others to adopt it. Clay paired "GTM engineer" with jobs, agencies and community. But terms get contested quickly. "AEO/GEO" has no clear owner, and Profound itself publishes definitions that separate the two terms.

### Cited Findings
- Clay's Series C was explicitly framed as funding "GTM engineering roles industrywide." The name became the fundraising and PR narrative itself. — [BusinessWire, 2025-08-05](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250805719448/en/AI-GTM-Leader-Clay-Raises-$100M-Series-C-to-Fuel-GTM-Engineering-Roles-Industrywide); [Clay "The GTM engineering era begins now"](https://www.clay.com/series-c)
- A critique says Clay did not invent the title, but its spending and promotion made it stick. The term appeared in outside job postings from March 2025, and the "costly signals" framing describes Clay investing visibly to legitimize the category. — [LymLyt](https://lymlyt.pro/blog/who-invented-gtm-engineer/); [Paul Syng](https://paulsyng.com/blog/clay-the-3-1b-category-creator-building-creativity-through-costly-signals/)
- Profound's blog draws a definitional line: "Generative Engine Optimization" names the technology, while "Answer Engine Optimization" names the behavior being optimized. Vendors are competing to own the *definition* of the category. — [Profound blog: best GEO tools](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/best-generative-engine-optimization-tools)
- G2 now has a formal "Answer Engine Optimization (AEO)" category, also known as GEO, with user reviews dated November 2026. The category has been institutionalized and is not owned by any one vendor. — [G2 AEO category](https://www.g2.com/categories/answer-engine-optimization-aeo)
- Competitor funding (sources vary):
  - Peec AI has raised about $29M, including a $21M Series A led by Singular.
  - Scrunch had about $19M from Mayfield and was then reportedly acquired by Sitecore for about $225M in June 2026 (single-source, unverified).
  - No AirOps funding figure was found.
  - — [Cairrot comparison](https://cairrot.com/blog/which-of-the-big-three-aeo-tools-is-best-profound-vs-peec-ai-vs-scrunch-ai-2026/); [Ayzeo GEO platforms compared](https://ayzeo.com/comparisons/geo-platforms-compared)
- Competitors publish "best AEO tools" listicles on their own blogs (Scrunch, Goodie, Profound). This is how vendors try to shape AI answers about the category. — [Scrunch blog](https://scrunch.com/blog/best-answer-engine-optimization-aeo-generative-engine-optimization-geo-tools-2026); [Goodie AI](https://higoodie.com/blog/best-aeo-software-tools/)
- "Services as software" is itself a VC-coined frame. Foundation Capital sizes it at $4.6T (secondary characterization; the primary text was not retrieved). Emergence describes AI-native services as selling outcomes, with margins that improve as models improve. — [Foundation Capital](https://foundationcapital.com/?p=10007); [Agent Market Cap summary of Foundation Capital's thesis](https://agentmarketcap.ai/blog/2026/04/11/foundation-capital-ai-investment-thesis-2026); [Emergence: "The Death of Deloitte"](https://www.emcap.com/thoughts/the-death-of-deloitte-ai-enabled-services-are-opening-a-whole-new-market)

### Inferences
- "AEO" is already a crowded, G2-standardized category, so Slate is unlikely to *own* it. The stronger move is to own a **sub-term or adjacent role** that Slate and TripleDart can credibly define. Examples: "autonomous marketing coworker," or a role name such as "AI search engineer" or "AEO engineer," mirroring "GTM engineer." Then back it with a job description template, a certification, salary data and an agency directory, the way Clay did.
- TripleDart's "services as software" framing borrows a VC thesis. That gives credibility with investors and founders, but it does not differentiate, because many AI-native services firms use it. It needs a proprietary name for TripleDart's *method*.
- Category terms matter for AI answers because LLMs answer "what is X / best X tools" queries from listicles and definitional pages. Whoever publishes the canonical definition, plus the most third-party mentions, tends to be named (see Question 3).

### Gaps
- No source establishes who coined "AEO," or any measured link between owning a term and AI-answer share.
- No quantitative evidence was found for how much category naming contributed to Clay's growth beyond correlation.

## 3. Data and research franchises, indexes, free tools and product-led content as brand moats

### Takeaway
Evidence suggests that *third-party mentions* of a brand drive AI visibility far more than owned content or backlinks. Data franchises work because they generate those earned citations: press, listicles and other people's posts. Gong Labs and the Profound Index show the model: use proprietary product data, publish it as a named benchmark, and launch it at an event.

### Cited Findings
- Ahrefs' study of 75,000 brands (December 2025, via secondary write-ups):
  - Branded web mentions correlated about 0.664 with AI Overview visibility, versus about 0.218 for backlinks.
  - YouTube mentions correlated about 0.737 in a later cut.
  - Brand search volume correlated about 0.334–0.392.
  - These are correlations, not causation. — [BrandMentions: 2026 evidence](https://brandmentions.com/blog/brand-mentions-ai-visibility/); [Digital Bloom 2025 AI visibility report](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/2025-ai-citation-llm-visibility-report/)
- An arXiv preprint (2606.25787) analyzed 167,551 citations for 128 brands across 12 markets. It found 85.7% of citations point to sites the brand does *not* own (14.3% owned), and 80% of citations come from about 18% of domains. — [arXiv 2606.25787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25787)
- AirOps (January 2026) found only about 30% of brands stay visible from one AI answer to the next for the same query. AI visibility is volatile. — [Digital Bloom report, citing AirOps](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/2025-ai-citation-llm-visibility-report/)
- A cross-engine study reportedly found that Perplexity never cites 89% of what ChatGPT cites for the same question, while engines agree on *brands* named about 4.5x more often than on exact pages (unverified against the original). — [Wellows: LLM citation trends](https://wellows.com/blog/llm-citation-trends-for-ai-search/)
- Measurement at scale: a 2026 arXiv paper studies GEO brand-visibility measurement across AI engines. — [arXiv 2606.20065](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.20065)
- Profound Index: 1.5B+ prompts and 50+ industries, branded as "the definitive benchmark," launched on stage at its own conference. [Company-reported] — [Profound newsroom](https://www.tryprofound.com/newsroom/profound-launches-the-profound-index-at-zero-click-new-york-creating-the-definitive-benchmark-for-ai-search-visibility)
- Gong Labs turns customer-call data into recurring research, which is repurposed for months and earns earned media. [Secondary] — [Wisp CMS](https://www.wisp.blog/blog/gong-content-marketing-case-study); [SalesFlint](https://salesflint.com/resources/brand-anatomy/gong-research-engine/)
- An agency example of product-led IP: Grow and Convert built its own AI-visibility tool, Traqer AI, and reports LLM brand recommendations for 50+ topics. The agency turned its methodology into software. [Listicle, secondary] — [Tenspeed: B2B growth agencies](https://www.tenspeed.io/blog/b2b-growth-agency)
- Claims that adding statistics lifts AI visibility (22%, from a vendor) or that GEO tactics lift it 30–40% (the Princeton GEO paper, via blogs) circulate mainly secondhand. Treat as unverified. — [Digital Bloom](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/2025-ai-citation-llm-visibility-report/)

### Inferences
- Slate Index's value as a brand moat depends less on its own SEO and more on how often *others* cite it: journalists, newsletter writers, agencies and LinkedIn creators. Design it to be quoted. Publish a monthly "top movers" list, one-line stats, per-category rankings and embeddable badges.
- TripleDart's 250+ clients are a data asset for a "Gong Labs"-style franchise. Aggregated, anonymized B2B SaaS benchmarks (for example, pipeline from AI search, CAC by channel, AEO lift by tactic) published annually would generate earned mentions. Those mentions correlate with AI visibility.
- Because AI answers are volatile (about 30% persistence), a recurring cadence (monthly or quarterly) beats a one-time annual report.

### Gaps
- No primary Ahrefs or Princeton GEO paper was fetched. The correlation figures are from secondary summaries.
- No direct evidence was found that a specific B2B index or report increased that company's AI-answer share.

## 4. B2B creator and influencer partnerships, employee advocacy and Thought Leader Ads: costs and results

### Takeaway
Thought Leader Ads (TLAs) and paid LinkedIn creators are the best-documented paid-brand lever in 2025–2026. Reported CTRs are several times higher than single-image ads, and CPCs are a fraction of theirs. But the benchmarks are vendor-sourced and vary widely, and TLAs still get under 10% of typical LinkedIn budgets.

### Cited Findings
- ZenABM's 2026 benchmarks show TLAs at a 2.68% median CTR versus 0.42% for single-image ads, and a $2.29 CPC versus $13.23. — [ZenABM LinkedIn ads benchmarks](https://zenabm.com/blog/linkedin-ads-benchmarks)
- LinkedIn's own reported figures are 252% higher CTR and 62% lower CPC for TLAs versus single-image posts. Other sources cite only a 1.6–1.7x lift, so the spread is large. — [Factors.ai TLA guide](https://www.factors.ai/blog/linkedin-thought-leader-ads); [Konabayev LinkedIn statistics 2026](https://konabayev.com/blog/linkedin-marketing-statistics-2026/)
- TLAs reportedly receive only 7–10% of the average LinkedIn budget, which suggests the format is under-allocated. — [Impactable TLA strategy](https://impactable.com/blog/the-ultimate-linkedin-thought-leadership-ads-strategy-for-b2b-marketers-2025/)
- Adoption, per a LinkedIn/Ipsos-based summary: 55% of B2B marketers already use creator marketing on LinkedIn, and 29% more plan to within a year. — [ContentGrip LinkedIn influencer playbook](https://www.contentgrip.com/linkedin-influencer-marketing-b2b-playbook/)
- Creator spend on LinkedIn reportedly grew 171% year over year, and 61% of B2B leaders plan to increase it. [Vendor] — [Favikon B2B influencer guide 2026](https://www.favikon.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-b2b-influencer-marketing)
- Creator economics:
  - Sponsored creator posts run as TLAs cost about 15–20% of the average LinkedIn CPM. — [Favikon](https://www.favikon.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-b2b-influencer-marketing)
  - Creators charge a 25–40% premium for paid-amplification rights, and returns diminish once paid exceeds about 50% of distribution. — [Clash.cc: TLAs vs B2B influencers](https://clash.cc/insights/linkedin-thought-leader-ads-vs-b2b-influencer)
- LinkedIn is reportedly "betting on B2B creators" (June 2026 commentary). — [Kaleigh Moore, 2026-06-08](https://www.kaleighmoore.com/blog/2026/6/8/linkedin-b2b-creators)
- HubSpot's creator program reportedly feeds its media acquisitions and is described as its largest source of organic content leads (secondhand). — [Rockwater newsletter](https://chris-erwin-rockwater-newsletter.beehiiv.com/p/hubspot-x-starter-story-the-case-for-saas-media-m-a)
- Profound's founder came from influencer marketing, which helps explain its creator- and event-heavy go-to-market. — [TechCrunch 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/13/move-over-seo-profound-is-helping-brands-with-ai-search-optimization/)

### Inferences
- The cheapest high-leverage play for a founder-led company is to run Shiyam's own posts, and those of TripleDart and Slate team members, as TLAs to the ICP. It is cheap per click and builds both the founder brand and the company brand.
- A "Slate creator program" fits two jobs. Pay B2B SaaS marketing creators to run live AI-visibility audits using Slate or Slate Index, and amplify the winners as TLAs. This produces content and the third-party mentions that feed AI visibility (Question 3).

### Gaps

- No primary LinkedIn TLA study was fetched. The figures come from vendor blogs.

## 7. How to measure brand in B2B now

### Takeaway
The working stack in 2025–2026 combines several signals: (1) a mandatory open-text "How did you hear about us?" field on demo forms, (2) branded search volume via Google Search Console, (3) direct traffic, (4) share of voice and visibility in AI answers across engines, tracked frequently because it is volatile, and (5) event-influenced pipeline and community metrics. Software attribution badly undercounts dark social, podcasts and LinkedIn.

### Cited Findings
- Refine Labs (2023, 620 conversions):
  - HubSpot software attribution credited web search with 78% of conversions, while buyers' self-reports credited it with 12%.
  - Dark social got roughly 0% of software attribution, but about 85–98% of self-reported answers or revenue, depending on the cut.
  - Refine sells this methodology, so it is promotional, and respondents could name multiple sources.
  - — [Refine Labs: Attribution mirage](https://www.refinelabs.com/blog/attribution-mirage); [Recast: HDYHAU limitations](https://getrecast.com/hdyhau/)
- Best practice for the question: use open text rather than a dropdown, make it required, and put it on the demo or booking form. Pair it with branded-search tracking in Google Search Console. — [Refine Labs](https://www.refinelabs.com/article/attribution-mirage); [Koji: self-reported attribution playbook](https://www.koji.so/blog/self-reported-attribution-interviews-marketing-playbook); [Cometly: measuring dark social](https://www.cometly.com/post/how-to-measure-dark-social)
- Recast argues HDYHAU has limits (recall bias, last-touch memory) and should be paired with modeling. — [Recast](https://getrecast.com/hdyhau/)
- Brand search volume correlates about 0.33–0.39 with AI visibility, so branded search is both a brand metric and a leading indicator for AI answers. — [BrandMentions](https://brandmentions.com/blog/brand-mentions-ai-visibility/)
- AI share of voice is volatile (about 30% of brands persist across consecutive answers, per AirOps) and differs by engine (Perplexity versus ChatGPT overlap is low). Track it across multiple engines with large prompt sets. — [Digital Bloom](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/2025-ai-citation-llm-visibility-report/); [Wellows](https://wellows.com/blog/llm-citation-trends-for-ai-search/)
- Commercial tooling is now standard: Profound tracks brand mentions, sentiment, competitor benchmarks and cited sources across "millions of real prompts." — [Lightspeed](https://lsvp.com/stories/profound-building-the-marketing-command-center-for-the-ai-era/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/24/exclusive-as-ai-threatens-search-profound-raises-96-million-to-help-brands-stay-visible/)
- Events: measure "event-influenced pipeline," since attendance alone measures activity, not impact. — [Unbound B2B](https://www.unboundb2b.com/blog/6-event-formats-that-still-drive-pipeline)
- Spend-data signals: Ramp's spend data was used as an external brand and growth signal (Granola ranked "second-fastest-growing software brand"). [Agency claim] — [Ragged Edge](https://raggededge.com/partnerships/granola)

### Inferences
- A practical brand scorecard for TripleDart and Slate, reviewed monthly:
  1. Share of open-text HDYHAU answers naming Shiyam, LinkedIn, podcasts, Slate Index, events or peers.
  2. Branded search impressions in Google Search Console for "TripleDart," "Slate HQ" and "Slate Index."
  3. Direct traffic.
  4. AI share of voice on a fixed prompt set (for example, "best GTM agency for B2B SaaS," "best AEO tool for agencies") across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude. Slate can dogfood this, which is itself a marketing story.
  5. Count of third-party mentions and citations of Slate Index.
  6. Event-influenced pipeline.
- Because Slate sells AI-visibility measurement, publishing TripleDart's and Slate's own brand scorecard openly ("building in public") doubles as product proof.

### Gaps
- No independent, non-vendor validation of the Refine Labs dark-social figures.
- No public benchmark for "good" AI share of voice in B2B SaaS.
- No data was found on community metrics, such as Clay Slack engagement-to-revenue linkage.

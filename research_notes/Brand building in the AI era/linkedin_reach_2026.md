# LinkedIn Organic Reach, Algorithm Changes and What B2B Audiences Engage With (2024 → Nov 2026)

> Research context: done for Shiyam Sunder (TripleDart / Slate), whose LinkedIn engagement used to be strong and is now much lower. Research date: 2026-11-02.
>
> **Method caveat (important for the report writer):** In this session the network blocked direct fetches of linkedin.com, news.linkedin.com, authoredup.com, socialinsider.io, socialmediatoday.com, creatorscience.com, ppc.land, techcrunch.com and stylemagazine.com. Most findings below therefore come from search-result summaries of those pages, not full-page reads. Exact figures should be spot-checked against the originals before publication. Nearly all "large-scale datasets" on LinkedIn reach come from tool vendors or consultants (van der Blom, AuthoredUp, Socialinsider, Metricool, Ordinal, ZenABM, Originality.ai). Each has a commercial interest, uses its own methodology, and many secondary blogs recycle their numbers. LinkedIn itself publishes no reach statistics.

## 1. What large-scale datasets say about organic reach and engagement trends, 2024 → 2026

### Takeaway
Every major dataset agrees that per-post reach/impressions for the median creator fell sharply between 2024 and 2026, by about 34% to 60% depending on dataset and window. Company pages were hit hardest. Engagement *rate* (engagements ÷ impressions) held flat or rose, so posts reach fewer people but the ones who see them interact more. Reach also became more concentrated: a small top tier gained while almost everyone else lost.

### Cited Findings
**Richard van der Blom, Algorithm Insights reports (the most-cited dataset)**
- 2025 report (about 1.8M posts, per secondary summaries): for about 95% of creators, reach "steadily declined," with a drop of nearly 50% by February 2025 vs. a year earlier. Recaps also cite views −50%, engagement −25%, follower growth −59%. — [Scribd copy of 2025 report ch.1](https://www.scribd.com/document/984921783/Algorithm-Insights-Report-2025-chapter-1-Richard-Van-der-Blom); [Mercer MacKay summary](https://mercermackay.com/thinking/blog/a-leaders-guide-to-the-linkedin-algorithm-what-the-data-says/)
- 2025 report: organic company-page reach reportedly fell to about 1% of followers, its lowest level in the data. Single-creator posts' share of top-performing posts rose from 27% to 39%. — [Scribd 2025 ch.1](https://www.scribd.com/document/984921783/Algorithm-Insights-Report-2025-chapter-1-Richard-Van-der-Blom) (via secondary summaries)
- 2026 report (1.3M posts from about 50,000 creators, per the Creator Science podcast #307 with Jay Clouse): reach is down about 60% for active creators over the last two years. — [Creator Science podcast #307](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/richard-van-der-blom-2/); [Spotify episode](https://open.spotify.com/episode/5BLtFJxMuDewrAS2j1PBEp)
- 2026 report, also on that podcast: about 80% of the comments van der Blom receives in the first five minutes of a post are AI-written. — [Creator Science podcast #307](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/richard-van-der-blom-2/) (via search summary)
- Posting frequency: secondary summaries of the van der Blom data say the optimal cadence fell from 5–6x/week to 2–4x/week, and that more than 3x/week starts to dilute per-post reach for most accounts. — [Carousels-generator summary](https://carousels-generator.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2026-carousels); [meet-lea summary](https://meet-lea.com/en/blog/linkedin-algorithm-explained). Note: sample sizes (1.3M vs 1.8M posts, 50k vs 58k profiles) are inconsistent across summaries.

**AuthoredUp (personal-profile tool vendor)**
- Analysis of 621,833 posts: average reach down 34%, and 98% of users saw a reach decline while the top ~2% gained. — [AuthoredUp algorithm post](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm) (via secondary summaries [contentin.io](https://contentin.io/blog/linkedin-content-statistics/), [socialpilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/?p=694640))
- A 3M+ post dataset gives median impressions of about 860 per post for personal profiles and about 500 for company pages. — [AuthoredUp metrics post](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-metrics-to-track) (via search summary)

**Socialinsider (business/company-page dataset)**
- 2026 LinkedIn benchmarks (1.3M business posts): average engagement rate 5.20%, **up 8% YoY**. Native documents lead at 7.00% (+14% YoY). The Q2 2026 update puts documents at 6.60%, down from 6.90% in Q1, and engagement was "mostly flat" in H1 2026. Video views on LinkedIn were **down 36% YoY**. — [Socialinsider 2026 LinkedIn benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/linkedin); [PDF](https://cdn.socialinsider.io/documents/linkedin_benchmarks_2026.pdf)

**Metricool**
- December 2025 study: LinkedIn impressions **−23%** and interactions **−14%** in 2025, while LinkedIn video impressions rose 73% and post clicks rose 31%. Metricool described "competition surging" on LinkedIn. — [Metricool 2026 study press release](https://metricool.com/press-release-2026-social-media-study/); [SMT press release, 2025-12-09](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/press-release/20251209-instagram-reels-decline-youtube-views-increase-competition-surges-on-link)
- An earlier 2025 trends study (577,180 posts) found video posts up 53% YoY, meaning far more video supply. — [Metricool LinkedIn trends study 2025](https://metricool.com/linkedin-trends-study-2025/)

**Other vendor datasets (weaker, single-source)**
- One analysis puts median impressions per post down about 47% over 12 months. — [Valley blog](https://www.joinvalley.co/blog/linkedin-360brew-algorithm-reach-dropped)
- Company-page organic reach reportedly fell 60–66% between 2024 and 2026. — [Ordinal company-page reach](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/the-declining-reach-of-linkedin-company-pages); [salesso roundup](https://salesso.com/blog/linkedin-organic-reach-statistics/)
- Average impressions per post reportedly down 63–66% since 2023, while engagement per post climbed 12–39% over the same window. — [socialpilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/?p=694640) (source of the underlying data unclear)
- Employee posts reportedly earn 2.75x more impressions and 5x more engagement than company-page posts (Refine Labs figure cited by a vendor; original date unclear). — [salesso roundup](https://salesso.com/blog/linkedin-organic-reach-statistics/)

**LinkedIn's own platform numbers (Microsoft earnings): engagement up, not down**
- LinkedIn's Q1 FY26 post (calendar Q3 2025) cited comments up 24% YoY and three consecutive quarters of double-digit growth in video uploads. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-reports-increase-in-post-comments-video-posts-microsoft-q1-2026/804353); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/410345/linkedin-touts-major-engagement-boosts-solid-ad-b.html)
- A Q4 FY25 summary reports comments up more than 30% and video uploads up about 20%. — [optimixed (SMT mirror)](https://www.optimixed.com/linkedin-reports-significant-increases-in-post-comments-and-video-posts/)
- Feb 2025: video uploads up 36% YoY as LinkedIn launched a vertical video feed. — [TechCrunch, 2025-02-04](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/04/linkedin-amps-up-vertical-video-tools-as-uploads-jump-36)
- July 2026: LinkedIn revenue up 12%, and Microsoft's CEO cited "strong engagement." — [Staffing Industry Analysts, 2026-07-29](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/linkedin-revenue-rises-12-microsoft-ceo-cites-strong-engagement); see also [LinkedIn Q3 2026 highlights](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/Q3-Earnings-Highlights) (not fetchable this session)

### Inferences
- The "collapse" founders feel is real at the median and is a **supply/demand effect plus a distribution-model change**, not just a penalty. Content supply rose (video posts +53%, comments +24–30%, more than half of long posts AI-assisted), while feed sessions did not grow as fast. Distribution also moved from "show to my network" to "show to people interested in the topic." Each post now competes against far more posts for the same feed slots.
- LinkedIn's platform-level growth in comments and video coexists with falling per-creator reach. This is consistent with a winner-take-more distribution: the 2% who gained vs. the 98% who lost in AuthoredUp's data.
- Rising engagement *rate* alongside falling impressions suggests the feed is narrowing distribution to more relevant viewers. Fewer but better-matched viewers can be fine for a B2B founder whose goal is pipeline, not vanity reach.
- A founder whose 2021–2023 reach depended on a broad network, frequent posting, and engagement from peers/pods (who are now filtered) would see a larger drop than the median.

### Gaps
- No primary access to the van der Blom 2025/2026 reports. Figures differ across summaries (−50% vs −60%, 1.3M vs 1.8M posts).
- No independent (non-vendor) or LinkedIn-published reach dataset exists.
- Buffer and Hootsuite 2025–2026 LinkedIn-specific reach studies were not found in this session. Hootsuite's statistics roundup appeared ([Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/linkedin-statistics/)) but could not be verified in detail.

## 2. What LinkedIn itself said and changed (ranking, AI, video, ads, creator programs)

### Takeaway
LinkedIn rebuilt its feed in 2025–2026 around LLM-embedding retrieval and a transformer sequential ranker. That pushes distribution toward topical/interest matching (what a post is about and who wrote it) and away from pure network graph. LinkedIn executives publicly committed to making engagement pods and automated comments "entirely ineffective" and to down-ranking low-quality AI filler. Commercially, LinkedIn is pushing video (BrandLink revenue share) and paid amplification of people's posts (Thought Leader Ads).

### Cited Findings
**Feed architecture (LinkedIn Engineering, primary but not fetched directly)**
- LinkedIn's engineering blog post "Engineering the next generation of LinkedIn's Feed" (March 12, 2026, credited to Hristo Danchev) describes a single unified retrieval pipeline built on LLM-generated embeddings. Retrieval is a dual encoder doing nearest-neighbor search in under 50 ms and passing about 2,000 candidates to the ranker. It replaced a multi-source retrieval system. — [ppc.land summary](https://ppc.land/linkedin-rebuilds-its-feed-from-scratch-with-llms-and-gpu-powered-ranking/); [ByteByteGo](https://blog.bytebytego.com/p/how-linkedin-feed-uses-llms-to-serve); [Grounded Hallucination](https://groundedhallucination.substack.com/p/how-linkedin-rebuilt-its-feed-recommendation)
- LLM-based retrieval reportedly reads each post's text and the author's headline/profile. That makes the author's stated topical identity part of matching. — [siela.in](https://siela.in/blog-linkedin-360brew-algorithm) (secondary)
- arXiv paper 2602.12354 (Feb 2026), "An Industrial-Scale Sequential Recommender for LinkedIn Feed Ranking": Feed SR, a transformer-based sequential ranking model, replaced the DCNv2-based ranker and showed **+2.10% time spent** in online A/B tests. — [arXiv 2602.12354](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12354v1)
- "360Brew" (Jan 2025 research paper; a 150B-parameter model built on Llama 3, covering 30+ tasks) is widely cited by marketers as "the new algorithm." LinkedIn's March 2026 posts reportedly do not name 360Brew as the production ranker, and one source says LinkedIn described it as research/pre-production. — [socialpilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/linkedin-algorithm); [suma-ai](https://www.suma-ai.com/en/blog/360brew-linkedin-algorithm); [leaders.social](https://www.leaders.social/linkedin-advanced-coaching/linkedin-360brew-visibility-engagement-reach). Treat "360Brew ranks your posts" as **unconfirmed marketer lore**. The sequential-ranker reading is backed by the arXiv paper.

**Pods, automation, engagement bait**
- Gyanda Sachdeva (LinkedIn VP Product Management): "Our goal is to make engagement pods entirely ineffective." LinkedIn uses behavioral signals to detect inauthentic interaction and said it would act on third-party tools (browser extensions, plug-ins) that automate commenting. Comments detected as automated are removed from the default "Most Relevant" comment view. — [Social Media Today: measures to combat engagement pods](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-outlines-more-measures-to-combat-engagement-pods/812290/); [Yahoo mirror](https://tech.yahoo.com/social-media/articles/linkedin-outlines-more-measures-combat-132759730.html)
- An earlier announcement said LinkedIn may limit the visibility of comments when it detects excessive comment creation or automation-tool use. — [Social Media Today: fake engagement](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-limit-visibility-of-comments-made-via-automation-tools/758207/); [LinkedLetter #166](https://thelinkedblog.com/2025/linkedletter-166-linkedin-expands-content-rules-and-cracks-down-on-fake-engagement-3719/)
- Coverage says LinkedIn reported a sharp rise in detected inauthentic activity in H1 2026 and is reducing visibility of recycled content, generic posts and obvious engagement bait. — [breakingsocialmedia.news, 2026](https://breakingsocialmedia.news/linkedin-steps-up-crackdown-on-ai-slop-and-fake-engagement/2026/) (secondary; original SMT article not fetched)

**AI-generated content**
- CNN (Clare Duffy), republished 2026-10-19: LinkedIn is pushing back on AI-generated filler. A team of former journalists and social media experts helps train its model to recognize authentic, useful posts. Posts the model flags as low-quality AI content are less likely to be distributed beyond the author's direct connections. — [Style Magazine / CNN, 2026-10-19](http://stylemagazine.com/news/2026/oct/19/even-linkedin-is-getting-tired-of-your-ai-written-posts/) (via search summary)
- LinkedIn is reportedly replacing its "enhance your post" AI rewrite feature, criticized for producing a generic AI voice, with a proofreading-only tool that keeps the author's voice. — [Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/how-to-use-ai-on-linkedin-while-sounding-human/)

**External links**
- No official "link penalty" exists. One source cites a LinkedIn product director saying there is no intentional link penalty if the post has standalone value, and LinkedIn's guidance says the feed only tapers low-quality, unsafe or spam content. — [Vivian Voss](https://www.vivianvoss.net/blog/linkedin-reach-you-did-not-earn) (secondary; quote not independently verified)

**Video and creator monetization**
- BrandLink began as the publisher-only Wire Program (June 2024) and was rebranded in May 2025 to include creators. It runs about 15-second pre-roll ads on creator/publisher videos with revenue share. Early results: 130% higher completion rates and 23% higher view rates than standard video ads. BrandLink revenue rose nearly 200% QoQ in the quarter ending June 30, and creator/publisher payouts more than tripled YoY. — [ppc.land: LinkedIn debuts BrandLink](https://ppc.land/linkedin-debuts-brandlink/); [MarTechView](https://martechview.com/linkedins-brandlink-video-ads-revenue-jumps-nearly-200/); [Reuters via TradingView](https://de.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com%2C2025%3Anewsml_L4N3UD13D%3A0-linkedin-deepens-video-ad-push-taps-more-publishers-and-creators-to-spur-growth)
- Vertical video feed launched alongside +36% YoY video uploads (Feb 2025). — [TechCrunch, 2025-02-04](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/04/linkedin-amps-up-vertical-video-tools-as-uploads-jump-36)

**Thought Leader Ads (paid amplification of members' posts)**
- LinkedIn-cited figure: Thought Leader Ads get 1.6x higher engagement than single-image ads. A Thought Leader Event Ads format links sponsored member posts to event pages. — [martech.org](https://martech.org/linkedin-the-marketers-guide/) (via summary)

### Inferences
- The architecture change explains the **"topic authority" effect** creators report. When retrieval embeds post text plus author profile/headline, a founder who posts on scattered topics, or whose headline does not match the content, matches fewer interested viewers. TripleDart's recent repositioning ("GTM engineering / services as software") could temporarily reduce topical match if the profile, headline and recent post history point to different topics.
- A sequential ranker optimized for time spent (+2.10% time spent was the reported win) structurally favors dwell: long-read text, document carousels, substantive comment threads. It disfavors quick-scroll bait.
- LinkedIn's commercial incentives (BrandLink video ads, Thought Leader Ads) mean organic reach is increasingly something you can top up with paid spend. Expect this direction to continue.

### Gaps
- LinkedIn's March 2026 engineering post and newsroom pages could not be read directly. Specific ranking signals (dwell weighting, comment-length weighting, "knowledge and advice" classifiers) are **not officially quantified** anywhere found.
- The exact phrase "knowledge and advice" was not found in a LinkedIn statement in this session.
- Creator mode removal (widely reported as March 2024) and newsletter-specific distribution changes were not verified in this session.
- Exact publication dates of the Sachdeva/pod articles were not visible (likely 2025–early 2026).

## 3. Is AI-generated content penalized, or just ignored by readers?

### Takeaway
Both, but the evidence for each is soft. LinkedIn says it trains models to limit distribution of low-quality AI filler beyond first-degree connections (CNN, Oct 2026). Originality.ai's studies show AI-likely posts underperforming human posts in most categories (−45% engagement in the 2024 study), with exceptions such as generic "leadership/inspiration" content. The penalty targets generic, low-value content, not AI assistance as such.

### Cited Findings
- Originality.ai 2025 follow-up: 3,368 long-form posts from 99 influential profiles. **53.7%** were classified as likely AI. AI posts beat human posts only in "Leadership & Inspiration" (2,635 vs 1,504 average interactions, a 75% gap). Human posts led in other categories; for example, human healthcare/medicine posts got 44% more engagement. — [Originality.ai LinkedIn AI engagement study](https://originality.ai/blog/linkedin-ai-study-engagement); [ppc.land coverage](https://ppc.land/over-half-of-linkedin-posts-are-now-likely-ai-but-authenticity-still-wins/)
- Originality.ai 2024 study: 8,795 long-form posts. Likely-AI posts received **45% less engagement** than likely-human posts. More than half of long posts have been likely AI since ChatGPT's launch. — [Originality.ai AI content on LinkedIn](https://originality.ai/blog/ai-content-published-linkedin)
- LinkedIn reportedly uses a team of former journalists/social experts to train models on "authentic, useful" posts. Flagged low-quality AI content is less likely to be distributed beyond direct connections. — [Style Magazine / CNN, 2026-10-19](http://stylemagazine.com/news/2026/oct/19/even-linkedin-is-getting-tired-of-your-ai-written-posts/)
- About 80% of first-five-minute comments on van der Blom's posts are AI-written. — [Creator Science podcast #307](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/richard-van-der-blom-2/)
- Vendor claims: low-effort AI content gets about 30% less reach and 55% less engagement, and generic AI content sees a 23–45% engagement drop; a "94% detection accuracy" figure also circulates. These are **unverified marketing-blog figures** with no traceable methodology. — [getviews.ai](https://getviews.ai/blog/does-linkedin-detect-ai-generated-content); [zoomsphere](https://www.zoomsphere.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2026-why-generic-ai-content-kills-your-organic-reach)
- Forbes (2025-03-20) lists recognizable "signs of AI content" on LinkedIn, a reader-side detection argument rather than data. — [Forbes / Jodie Cook](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jodiecook/2025/03/20/7-signs-of-ai-generated-content-on-linkedin/)

### Inferences
- With more than half of long-form posts AI-generated and AI comments flooding first-hour engagement, a recognizably personal, specific, first-hand voice is now *scarce* and differentiated. The feed's own quality classifiers and readers' pattern-matching likely both punish sameness.
- For Slate (an AI marketing agent product), this matters for positioning. The winning pitch is AI that preserves the operator's voice and inserts proprietary data/stories, not AI that writes generic posts. That pitch matches the direction of LinkedIn's own tool changes (enhance → proofread-only).
- AI-written comments on others' posts are likely filtered out of "Most Relevant," so comment-led growth only works if comments are human and substantive.

### Gaps
- Originality.ai sells the detector it used, and there is no independent replication. Engagement comparisons don't control for author, topic or audience size.
- No controlled study isolates "AI-written but high-quality" vs. "human-written" performance.
- LinkedIn has not quantified any AI-content demotion.

## 4. Which formats and topics perform best now

### Takeaway
On personal profiles, **document carousels** consistently lead on both reach and engagement (AuthoredUp, about 1.39x reach and 1.30x engagement vs. average, Mar 2025–Feb 2026), followed by images. Polls get reach but little engagement. Video, despite LinkedIn's push, underperforms per post for most creators (0.86x reach), and its views fell 36% YoY on business pages, likely because supply exploded. Reshares and LinkedIn Articles are the weakest. Longer, substantive text beats short posts. Topic-level evidence is mostly anecdotal, but data points toward specific, first-hand, expertise-led content.

### Cited Findings
**Format performance, personal profiles (AuthoredUp, Mar 2025–Feb 2026, relative to average post)**
- Documents/carousels: 1.39x reach, 1.30x engagement, only 4.88% of posts. Among the top 5% of profiles: 1.72x reach, 1.63x engagement. — [AuthoredUp: Best performing content 2026](https://authoredup.com/blog/best-performing-content-on-linkedin); [influensea summary](https://www.influensea.com/blog/best-linkedin-post-formats)
- Images: 1.20x reach, 1.33x engagement, 57.16% of posts. — same
- Polls: 1.78x reach but 0.37x engagement ("a reach trap"). — same
- Text-only: 1.07x reach, 0.78x engagement. — same
- Video: 0.86x reach, 0.93x engagement. — same
- Articles: 0.69x reach, 0.44x engagement. Reshares: 0.29x reach, 0.22x engagement. — same
- Median document reach fell 25% in one 2025 AuthoredUp article even though documents still lead relatively. **All multipliers are relative and do not mean absolute reach is rising.** — [AuthoredUp post statistics](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-post-statistics) (via summary)
- Post length: 20+ sentence posts get 1.14x reach and 1.18x engagement vs. format average. Posts under 5 sentences get 0.64x reach. — [AuthoredUp: how to increase engagement](https://authoredup.com/blog/how-to-increase-engagement-on-linkedin)
- Frequency: 4–5 posts/week had the highest engagement rate and 28% more impressions per post than once weekly (AuthoredUp). This **conflicts** with van der Blom's 2–4x/week recommendation. — [AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/how-to-increase-engagement-on-linkedin); [carousels-generator summary of vdB](https://carousels-generator.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2026-carousels)

**Format performance, company pages (Socialinsider 2026)**
- Native documents have the highest engagement rate (7.00%, +14% YoY; 6.60% in Q2 2026). Video views are down 36% YoY. — [Socialinsider 2026 benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/linkedin)

**Hashtags and links**
- Posts with more than 3 hashtags reportedly performed 71% worse than posts with none. — [socialpilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/?p=694640) (single vendor source)
- Link penalty estimates vary widely:
  - van der Blom (about 1.3M posts): one external link in the body cuts median reach by **18.8%**. A later update reportedly shows 4+ links about 24% below baseline. — [Neal Schaffer newsletter](https://newsletter.nealschaffer.com/posts/linkedin-links-cost-you-18-8-reach); [magicpost](https://magicpost.in/de/blog/linkedin-external-links-reach)
  - Ordinal (900k+ posts): average penalty 26.5%, rising from about 5% (2023) to 42% (2025); personal profiles were "largely immune" vs. company pages. — [Ordinal link-penalty study](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/linkedin-link-penalty-study)
  - Agility PR claims about 60%. — [Agility PR / Bulldog Reporter](https://www.agilitypr.com/pr-news/social-media-influencer-marketing/the-linkedin-link-tax-why-sharing-your-blog-is-costing-you-60-percent-of-your-audience/)
  - All are correlational, and one Q1 2026 analysis (methodology unpublished) claimed multi-link posts did better.

**Topics (thin evidence)**
- Originality.ai: human-written, domain-specific posts (e.g., healthcare) outperform AI posts, while generic inspiration content does not differentiate. — [Originality.ai](https://originality.ai/blog/linkedin-ai-study-engagement)
- Creators told SAN (Straight Arrow News) that posts on certain sensitive/political topics (e.g., the "Cornell 7") reached significantly fewer people, with no explanation from LinkedIn. — [SAN](https://san.com/cc/they-posted-about-the-cornell-7-on-linkedin-then-their-numbers-dropped/)
- Kyle Poyar's style is analyzed (by an AI-writing vendor) as built on "concrete numbers, timeframes, named examples," which is anecdotal. — [ViralBrain](https://www.viralbrain.ai/blog/kyle-poyar-kyle-poyar-backlog-playbook-b2b)

### Inferences
- For a B2B GTM founder, the best-evidenced combination is a long-form text post or document carousel carrying **proprietary data or a concrete teardown**, posted 2–5x/week on a consistent topic cluster, with no hashtag stuffing and links used sparingly or not at all.
- Video is a supply-glutted format. LinkedIn wants it for ad inventory (BrandLink), but per-post returns for a typical founder are below average unless video is a genuine strength.
- Polls inflate impressions without real attention. Avoid them if the goal is pipeline and mindshare.

### Gaps
- No rigorous dataset comparing *topic types* (original data vs. personal story vs. contrarian take vs. behind-the-scenes vs. teardown). Evidence here is anecdote.
- No data on "dwell-time" by format from LinkedIn.
- Vendor datasets conflict on optimal frequency.

## 5. Comments, DMs, dark social, employee advocacy and Thought Leader Ads

### Takeaway
Reach now depends more on early, substantive conversation than on reactions. LinkedIn reports platform comments up 24–30%+ YoY and is filtering automated/pod comments out of default views. Personal and employee posts significantly out-reach company pages. Thought Leader Ads are the paid bridge, though their headline CTR advantage largely reflects LinkedIn counting social clicks.

### Cited Findings
- LinkedIn reports comments up 24% YoY (Q1 FY26) and more than 30% (Q4 FY25). — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-reports-increase-in-post-comments-video-posts-microsoft-q1-2026/804353); [optimixed](https://www.optimixed.com/linkedin-reports-significant-increases-in-post-comments-and-video-posts/)
- van der Blom: comments and instant reposts outperform other engagement actions, and the first hour after posting strongly shapes a post's reach. — [meet-lea summary](https://meet-lea.com/en/blog/linkedin-algorithm-explained); [carousels-generator](https://carousels-generator.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2026-carousels) (secondary)
- Automated comments are removed from "Most Relevant," and excessive commenting/automation can limit comment visibility. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-outlines-more-measures-to-combat-engagement-pods/812290/)
- Employee posts get 2.75x the impressions and 5x the engagement of company-page posts (Refine Labs, via vendor). Personal profiles reportedly get about 65% of feed distribution. Company-page organic reach is down 60–66% from 2024 to 2026. — [salesso](https://salesso.com/blog/linkedin-organic-reach-statistics/); [Ordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/the-declining-reach-of-linkedin-company-pages)
- Thought Leader Ads benchmarks (ZenABM, 2026; 211 B2B companies, 161,256 ads): TLA CTR 2.68%, CPC $2.29, CPM $49.37, vs. single-image median CTR 0.42%, CPC $13.23, and video CTR 0.24%. Top TLAs run 4.49–17.39% CTR. — [ZenABM TLA benchmarks](https://zenabm.com/blog/tla-benchmarks/); [ZenABM 2026 benchmarks](https://zenabm.com/blog/linkedin-ads-benchmarks-2026)
- Counter-evidence: a September 2026 Kiin Labs paired within-account benchmark found landing-page CTR of 0.449% (TLA) vs 0.441% (company-page Sponsored Content), roughly parity. Understory notes LinkedIn's TLA "CTR" counts reactions/comments/shares as clicks, and ZenABM notes CTR slightly *negatively* correlated with pipeline. — [Understory](https://www.understoryagency.com/blog/linkedin-thought-leader-ads-vs-brand-ads)
- LinkedIn-cited: Thought Leader Ads get 1.6x higher engagement than single-image ads. — [martech.org](https://martech.org/linkedin-the-marketers-guide/)
- Koka Sexton (SignalScout) recommends tracking comments from named target accounts as warm buying signals. — [SignalScout newsletter](https://signalscout.kokasexton.com/newsletter/founder-led-growth-linkedin)
- Audience fit beats size: one vendor says a single niche expert with 176 engagers produced more qualified leads than 16 other profiles combined. — [Linkboost](https://www.linkboost.co/blog/how-founders-grew-linkedin-following-2026/) (vendor anecdote)

### Inferences
- Commenting thoughtfully on posts by ICP buyers and adjacent creators is a reach channel in its own right: comments surface to their networks. But it only works if comments are clearly human and add substance, since automated ones are filtered.
- For TripleDart, a workable stack is organic posts on the founder profile, team/employee posts, and TLAs on the best-performing organic posts (proven content → paid distribution to ICP), measured on pipeline rather than LinkedIn's inflated TLA CTR.
- "Dark social" (DMs, screenshots, forwarding) is not measurable in any dataset found. Anecdotally it is where B2B attention converts, so pipeline attribution should use self-reported attribution ("how did you hear about us").

### Gaps
- No public data quantifies DM/dark-social flows from LinkedIn content.
- No LinkedIn data on how much reach comments on others' posts generate.
- Employee advocacy figures (2.75x/5x) are an older Refine Labs number with unclear date.

## 6. Examples of B2B founders/creators who grew reach in 2025–2026

### Takeaway
Well-documented, data-backed founder case studies from 2025–2026 are scarce. Most available "case studies" are agency/vendor marketing. The credible signals: Kyle Poyar calls LinkedIn his single best acquisition channel (June 2025), Pylon reportedly gets its biggest pipeline source from LinkedIn, and Storylane's VP Marketing credits LinkedIn with more than 50% of pipeline via founder-led branding plus employee advocacy. The shared pattern is niche topical consistency, ICP-specific problems, concrete numbers/examples, and team-wide posting.

### Cited Findings
- Kyle Poyar (Growth Unhinged, June 2025, paywalled) says LinkedIn became his single best acquisition channel, and cites Pylon, where LinkedIn is the biggest source of pipeline. — [Growth Unhinged: guide to mastering LinkedIn](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/guide-to-mastering-linkedin)
- Storylane's VP of Marketing publicly credits LinkedIn with more than 50% of sales pipeline via systematic founder-led personal branding and employee advocacy. — [Linkboost founders case studies](https://www.linkboost.co/blog/how-founders-grew-linkedin-following-2026/) (secondary)
- Vendor case studies, all self-reported: a consultant grew from 312 to about 2,500 followers in 90 days, filling a high-ticket retainer pipeline; HHC Growth reports 34.5% follower growth in 90 days and 26–43% connection acceptance. — [Linkboost](https://www.linkboost.co/blog/how-founders-grew-linkedin-following-2026/); [HHC Growth](https://hhcgrowth.lovable.app/case-studies/linkedin-growth-system)
- RevBoss founder-led marketing benchmark covers 1,202 founder posts (Jan–Aug 2026); findings were not visible in this session. — [RevBoss benchmarks](https://www.revboss.com/founder-led-marketing-benchmarks.md)
- Teardown of "5 LinkedIn systems generating 8-figure B2B pipelines" (Substack; not read). — [startupgtm Substack](https://startupgtm.substack.com/p/5-linkedin-systems-generating-8-figure)

### Inferences
- The founders who grew in 2025–2026 appear to treat LinkedIn as a **pipeline channel with a narrow ICP**, not a reach game: consistent topic, concrete operator data, team amplification, and often paid boosts. That fits the algorithm shift toward topical matching.
- For Shiyam, the best-fit move is probably publishing TripleDart's proprietary GTM data (client benchmarks, experiments, teardowns of GTM-engineering workflows) under one tightly defined topic identity.

### Gaps
- No primary, quantified 2025–2026 case studies of named B2B founders (follower/impression time series) were found. Kyle Poyar's post is paywalled, and Lenny Rachitsky material on LinkedIn growth was not found.
- Reddit r/linkedin and r/marketing threads did not surface in searches. Creator-anecdote coverage relied on blogs and podcasts instead.

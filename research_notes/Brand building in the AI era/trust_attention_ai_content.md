## 1. How much content is AI-generated, and how do readers react (detect, distrust, disengage)?

### Takeaway
About half of new English web articles and about half of long LinkedIn posts are now likely AI-written (2024–2025 detector studies). Readers are not good at spotting AI, but when they *suspect* it, trust drops by roughly half, whoever actually wrote it. The penalty comes from the perception of AI, so content that looks generic gets penalized even if a human wrote it.

### Cited Findings

**Volume**
- Graphite (growth agency) with GPTZero sampled 65,000 English article URLs from CommonCrawl (Jan 2020–May 2025). AI-generated articles overtook human-written ones around **November 2024**. As of May 2025 the split was **51.7% AI / 48.3% human**, and the share has **plateaued around 50%** since about Q1 2025 (one GPTZero passage dates the plateau to May 2024 instead, so the sources disagree). Method: Surfer AI detector on 500-word chunks; 4.2% false-positive rate on pre-ChatGPT articles; 99.4% recall, tested only on GPT-4o output. — [Stacker/ABC17, Oct 23 2025](https://abc17news.com/stacker-science/2025/10/23/more-articles-are-now-created-by-ai-than-humans/); [GPTZero](https://gptzero.me/news/ai-written-web/)
- Graphite's own caveat: a high share of AI articles being *published* does not mean a similar share of *traffic*. A companion Graphite study found these AI articles **largely don't appear in Google and ChatGPT results**. — [GPTZero](https://gptzero.me/news/ai-written-web/) (secondary summary)
- Originality.ai (Nov 2024): of 8,795 long-form LinkedIn posts (100+ words), **54%** were estimated AI-generated as of Oct 2024. — [The Register, Nov 28 2024](https://www.theregister.com/2024/11/28/linkedin_ai_posts); [Originality.ai](https://originality.ai/pt/blog/ai-content-published-linkedin)
- Originality.ai 2025 update: 3,368 long posts published in 2025 by 99 influential profiles; **53.7% likely AI**. In **marketing and branding, human posts performed 73% better**. AI posts did slightly better in leadership and finance. — [Originality.ai](https://originality.ai/blog/linkedin-ai-study-engagement); [PPC Land summary](https://ppc.land/over-half-of-linkedin-posts-are-now-likely-ai-but-authenticity-still-wins/)
- One secondary write-up says likely-AI LinkedIn posts got **45% less engagement** than likely-human posts in Originality's data (not verified against the primary source). — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/over-half-of-linkedin-posts-are-now-likely-ai-but-authenticity-still-wins/)
- Counter-estimate: Pangram Labs flagged **41%** of long LinkedIn posts as *fully* AI-generated. LinkedIn told The Register the flagged content was "AI-assisted," not necessarily fully AI-generated. — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2024/11/28/linkedin_ai_posts); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/over-half-of-linkedin-posts-are-now-likely-ai-but-authenticity-still-wins/)

**Detection and reaction**
- Pew (survey of 5,023 US adults, June 9–15 2025; published Sept 17 2025): **76%** say it is extremely or very important to be able to tell whether pictures, videos and text were made by AI or by people, but **53%** are not too or not at all confident they can tell. **53%** say AI will make people worse at thinking creatively (16% say better). — [Pew Research Center, Sept 17 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2025/09/17/how-americans-view-ai-and-its-impact-on-people-and-society/)
- Pew: if they learned a painting they liked was AI-made, **49%** would like it less and 48% would not change their view. Among under-30s, **66%** would like it less, compared with 36% of those 65+. In other words, younger audiences carry a bigger AI penalty. — [Pew short read, Sept 17 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/09/17/from-political-speeches-to-songs-how-would-americans-react-if-they-found-out-ai-was-involved/); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/09/17/pew-research-american-ai-survey-2025)
- Raptive (published July 15 2025; 3,000 US adults; articles across 5 verticals including travel, food, finance and parenting; identical content labeled "AI" or "human" for split groups): trust fell **about 50%** when readers suspected AI involvement, **even when a human wrote the piece**. Ads next to suspected-AI content saw about a **14%** drop in brand ad performance (purchase consideration and willingness to pay a premium). Coverage calls this "AI stink." — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/raptive-study-shows-ai-content-cuts-reader-trust-by-half/); [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/raptive-study-ai-stink/). Caveat: secondary coverage only; I could not reach the full Raptive report or fetch the PPC Land page.
- Gartner consumer survey (March 2026, n=307 US consumers): **49%** agree GenAI has made the quality of available content worse, and AI is contributing to a more skeptical media environment. — [Gartner, via search summary of Gartner newsroom coverage](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-20-gartner-survey-finds-sixty-nine-percent-of-b-two-b-buyers-turn-to-sales-reps-to-validate-ai-generated-insights) (small sample; exact release URL for the consumer survey not confirmed)

**News audiences (Reuters Institute)**
- Digital News Report 2025 (published June 17 2025; 48 markets): there is a "clear and persistent comfort gap" between human-led and AI-led news production. The public draws a sharp line between back-end AI uses (acceptable) and front-facing AI uses (not). — [Reuters Institute DNR 2025 PDF](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2025-06/Digital_News-Report_2025.pdf); [Journalism.co.uk](https://www.journalism.co.uk/what-the-public-wants-seven-opportunities-for-newsrooms-in-the-age-of-generative-ai/)
- More than half of people are uncomfortable with news produced mostly by AI. — [Longford Leader, citing Reuters Institute](https://www.longfordleader.ie/news/national-news/1531529/more-than-half-of-people-uncomfortable-with-news-produced-mostly-by-ai-report.html) (secondary)
- Digital News Report 2026 (published June 16 2026): global trust in news is **37%**, the lowest since measurement began in 2015, and down in 29 of 48 markets. Only **20%** trust AI-derived news answers. **10%** use AI chatbots for news weekly across markets (up from 7% in 2025), rising to 16% of under-35s. — [Reuters Institute DNR 2026 PDF](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2026-06/DNR%202026%20FINAL_2.pdf); [Benton](https://www.benton.org/headlines/reuters-institute-digital-news-report-2026); [PR Daily](https://www.prdaily.com/the-new-news-reality-bigger-reach-lower-trust/) (figures from secondary coverage; PDF not opened)

### Inferences
- Supply of competent-looking text has roughly doubled, but distribution (Google, ChatGPT citations) does not seem to reward it (per Graphite). Producing more of it has diminishing or negative returns.
- Raptive's finding is the key one for a B2B founder. The cost lands on content that *looks* AI-made, so "everyone's content looks the same" is itself a trust tax, whether or not AI wrote it. Distinctiveness is how you avoid that penalty.
- People can't reliably detect AI, so they fall back on heuristics: generic structure, no specifics, no named person, no first-hand detail. Signals that defeat those heuristics matter more than disclosure.

### Gaps
- No primary Ahrefs study on the AI-content share was found in this pass (Ahrefs has published one in 2025 per general knowledge, but I did not verify it). It is left out.
- No Nielsen Norman Group 2025–2026 study on AI-content perception was located in this pass.
- The full Raptive methodology and verbatim quotes are unverified (fetch blocked).

## 2. What signals trust now? Evidence by signal

### Takeaway
The best-supported trust signals in 2025–2026 are **proximity and peers** (Edelman 2026: trust is concentrating in "my" circle), **peer reviews and third-party validation** (TrustRadius, G2), **visible human authorship** (Raptive, Pew, Reuters comfort gap), and, more tentatively, **verifiable provenance** (C2PA labels raise trust in experiments, but people often misunderstand them). The evidence for "original data," "taste," and "consistency over time" is mostly indirect or comes from practitioners.

### Cited Findings
- **Proximity/insularity (Edelman Trust Barometer 2026; released ~Jan 18–20 2026; 33,938 respondents, 28 countries):** the theme is "insularity," which Richard Edelman calls "the next crisis of trust." Trust is concentrating in close circles: **My CEO 66%, my fellow citizens 64%, my neighbors 64%**. About **70%** are hesitant or unwilling to trust someone with different values or backgrounds. "My Employer" is the only institution with majority agreement (**58%**) that it is bridging divides. — [Edelman press release](https://www.edelman.com/news-awards/2026-edelman-trust-barometer-society-slides-into-insularity); [Richard Edelman blog](https://www.edelman.com/newsroom/richard-edelmans-6am-blog/insularity-next-crisis-trust). (A 78% "my employer" trust figure appears in secondary sources but was not verified, so it is left out.)
- **Peer reviews still close B2B deals (TrustRadius 2026 "B2B Buying Disconnect," released July 15 2026; 1,862 buyers + 444 vendors, fieldwork Jan 2026):** 63% of buyers use AI in the purchase journey, but **94% fact-check AI responses** at least some of the time. **74%** use reviews to inform purchases. AI speeds research but "has not supplanted peer reviews, demos, or trials," and AI recommendations are themselves heavily shaped by third-party content (reviews, independent publications, peer accounts). — [Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/news-brief/trustradius-ai-has-changed-how-buyers-research-but-not-what-they-trust/53853/); [MarketScale](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/software-and-technology/94-of-b2b-buyers-fact-check-ai-research-and-peer-reviews-still-close-the-deal) (secondary)
- **Gen Z relies more on social proof:** TrustRadius 2025 found Gen Z buyers are more influenced by user reviews, peer conversations and forums than older generations. — [TrustRadius "Bridging the Trust Gap"](https://solutions.trustradius.com/vendor-blog/bridging-the-trust-gap-b2b-tech-buying-in-the-age-of-ai)
- **Humans as validators (Gartner, May 20 2026):** **69%** of B2B buyers prefer to validate AI-generated insights with sales reps. 51% say they are more likely to get misleading information from GenAI, and 49% say the same of a sales rep. — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-20-gartner-survey-finds-sixty-nine-percent-of-b-two-b-buyers-turn-to-sales-reps-to-validate-ai-generated-insights)
- **Human authorship / perceived humanness:** perceived AI involvement halves trust (Raptive 2025). Reuters finds audiences accept AI behind the scenes but resist front-facing AI. — [PPC Land on Raptive](https://ppc.land/raptive-study-shows-ai-content-cuts-reader-trust-by-half/); [Reuters Institute DNR 2025](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2025-06/Digital_News-Report_2025.pdf)
- **Thought leadership vs. brand recognition (Edelman–LinkedIn 2025 B2B Thought Leadership Impact Report, 7th annual, ~2,000 management-level respondents):** **53%** of decision-makers say that when a company's thought leadership is strong, brand recognition matters less. Secondary sources also report that 64% spend over an hour a week reading thought leadership, and that over 40% of deals stall from buying-group misalignment. — [Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/news-brief/marketers-need-to-gear-content-to-hidden-b2b-buyers-edelman-linkedin/49901/); [Edelman](https://www.edelman.com/news-awards/new-research-edelman-linkedin-thought-leadership-influences-b2b-sales) (some figures secondary and inconsistently attributed)
- **Provenance (C2PA):** an ICWSM study (Trattner et al., listed 2026) found C2PA provenance labels increase trust in digital news platforms across Western countries. — [ICWSM/AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/42749). A CEUR workshop study (N=202) found labels raised *article* trust but not image trust or article selection, and many participants misread what the labels meant. — [CEUR-WS](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4056/short4.pdf). Norway's Project Reynir (~2,000 news consumers, 3 countries) found problems with icon recognition and placement. — [Kaptur on IPTC summit](https://kaptur.co/content-provenance-grows-up-four-signals-from-the-iptc-toronto-summit/). A critique: C2PA only shows who signed a content history claim, not whether the content is true. — [arXiv 2604.24890](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24890)
- **Rand Fishkin (SparkToro) on single AI answers:** "After doing this research, I don't trust one AI answer. I refresh multiple times. Any single response is incomplete." — [Near Media EP 244](https://nearmedia.co/ep-244-ai-visibility-vs-google-rankings-rand-fishkin-on-the-future-of-search-zero-click-brand-measurement) (quote as given in the episode summary; listed as 2026)

### Inferences
- The signals that work all **attach content to an accountable, verifiable entity**: a named person, a peer, a real customer, a signed provenance record. Anonymous brand voice is the worst position to be in.
- Edelman's insularity data suggests trust flows through people buyers already feel close to (their own CEO, peers, communities). For a founder, that points toward the founder's own voice and customers' voices, not polished "brand" content.
- Buyers already treat AI output as a draft to verify. Content that *is* the verification layer (reviews, real numbers, named customers) is what gets checked against, and what AI assistants cite.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous 2025–2026 study that isolates "original data," "consistency over time," or "taste/curation" as causal trust drivers. The case for them comes from thinkers and practitioners, not controlled studies.
- No study found on live or unscripted formats (livestreams, unedited podcasts) as trust signals in the AI era.

## 3. Economic framing from credible thinkers: abundance vs. scarcity of trust and attention

### Takeaway
Credible analysts agree: when creation is free, value moves to **provenance, authenticity, and the human identity behind content** (Ben Thompson), and to **influence inside platforms rather than clicks** (Rand Fishkin). The "taste is the moat" and "trust is the last scarce asset" theses are everywhere in 2025 Substack writing, but mostly from lesser-known authors. I could not retrieve verbatim quotes from Paul Graham, Packy McCormick, Eugene Wei, Li Jin, Julian Shapiro, Dan Koe or Gary Vee in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- **Ben Thompson, "AI and the Human Condition" (Stratechery, 2026), paraphrased from search summary because direct fetch was blocked:** LLM output is individualized, so it cannot provide the *shared* content a common audience values. The spread of high-quality AI art will make human art *more* valuable because of its provenance. "Humans will still want humans," which he frames as a labor economy that exists because the work is human. — [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2026/ai-and-the-human-condition/)
- **Ben Thompson, "AI, NIL, and Zero Trust Authenticity" (Stratechery, 2023), paraphrased:** as AI content grows, authenticity matters more. The answer is to *verify what is authentic* rather than try to eliminate AI content, and he ties this to name, image and likeness rights. — [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2023/ai-nil-and-zero-trust-authenticity/)
- **Ben Thompson, Jan 2026 rebuttal to Dwarkesh Patel / Philip Trammell:** people will still prefer human-created content and human connection even if AI can do all human jobs. — [Slashdot summary](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/26/01/06/0915233/stratechery-pushes-back-on-ai-capital-dystopia-predictions)
- **Ben Thompson, "Content and Community" (Stratechery, 2025):** on abundance and aggregation (content not retrieved). — [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2025/content-and-community/)
- **Ben Thompson on Cheeky Pint (Stripe) podcast:** some content may become more valuable than ever because AI is a perfectly individualized experience. He floats crypto as an authenticity identifier in a world of "infinite content." — [Cheeky Pint](https://cheekypint.substack.com/p/ben-thompson-from-stratechery-on)
- **Rand Fishkin (SparkToro), "zero-click" framing:** "Discovery and audience influence are moving onto platforms. 'Don't build on rented land' is no longer viable advice. You must build where attention exists." — [Near Media EP 244](https://nearmedia.co/ep-244-ai-visibility-vs-google-rankings-rand-fishkin-on-the-future-of-search-zero-click-brand-measurement). Marketers need new frameworks to prove impact when discovery happens without a website visit. — [TopRank Beyond B2B #22](https://www.toprankmarketing.com/podcast/beyond-b2b-22-rand-fishkin/). Fuller argument: book *Zero Click Marketing* (with Amanda Natividad).
- Zero-click stat (secondhand): a SparkToro study reportedly found **68%** of Google searches ended without a click in Jan–Apr 2026 (about 60% in 2024). — [Zero Click Marketing podcast](https://share.transistor.fm/s/04446b98) (unverified against SparkToro primary)
- **G2 "Answer Economy" framing:** "Don't win the click — win the answer." — [G2](https://company.g2.com/news/g2-research-the-answer-economy)
- **The Substack consensus (lower authority):** "Trust Gravity" (March 2025) argues that when AI makes everything abundant, trust will be the only thing that stays scarce. — [Anti-Fragile Essays](https://antifragilehuman.substack.com/p/trust-gravity-will-rule-content-economy). Another essay lays out a sequence: printing made information abundant, so attention became scarce; the internet made distribution abundant, so trust became scarce; AI makes creation abundant, so judgment becomes scarce. — [TheGreySwan](https://thegreyswan.substack.com/p/the-age-of-abundance). "Taste is the new moat." — [The VC Corner](https://www.thevccorner.com/p/why-taste-is-the-new-moat). "Strategy becomes subtraction." — [The Brand Algorithm](https://www.the-brand-algorithm.com/content-strategy-in-the-age-of-ai/)
- **Counterpoint:** a commentary on Hito Steyerl's "derivative image" treats AI-era "taste" as parasitic, not a new human superpower. That is a caution against using "taste" as a slogan. — (cited within [Geraldine Wharry](https://www.geraldinewharry.com/getinspired/taste-economy-the-last-intimacy))

### Inferences
- The strongest economic logic: AI makes **output** abundant and leaves **accountability** (a named human staking reputation), **provenance** (it really happened), and **shared reference points** (something everyone in a niche read) scarce. Thompson's point that individualized AI output can't create shared content suggests that *flagship, canonical* pieces (an annual report everyone in the category cites) gain value.
- Fishkin's zero-click framing plus G2's "win the answer" mean the unit of success moves from traffic to *being the source an AI or peer repeats*. That favors content that is quotable and contains facts that exist nowhere else.

### Gaps
- No verbatim, sourced 2025–2026 quotes were retrieved from Paul Graham, Packy McCormick, Eugene Wei ("Status as a Service" is from 2019), Li Jin, Julian Shapiro, Dan Koe or Gary Vee. Do not attribute specific quotes to them without verification.
- Stratechery pages could not be fetched (domain blocked), so the Thompson items are paraphrases from search summaries, not verbatim. stratechery.com and ppc.land would have to be allowed in the session network settings to pull exact quotes.

## 4. B2B buyer research: how buyers discover and shortlist vendors (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
B2B buyers decide before they talk to you. In about 95% of cases the winner is already on the day-one shortlist (6sense 2025). AI chatbots have become the **#1 shortlist influence** (G2 2026: 54%, ahead of review sites at 43%). Buyers still verify through peers, reviews and reps, and AI answers are themselves built from third-party and peer content. So brand presence *before* the buying cycle, plus third-party validation, decides who makes the list.

### Cited Findings
- **6sense 2025 B2B Buyer Experience Report (~4,000 buyers):** sellers are first contacted about **61%** of the way through the journey (down from 69%, about 6–7 weeks earlier). **79%** of buyers initiate first contact. Buyers contact their top-ranked vendor first about **80%** of the time and buy from it **77%** of the time. **95%** of the time the winner is on the day-one shortlist. Average cycle length fell from 11.3 months (2024) to 10.1 months (2025). — [6sense report page](https://6sense.com/b2b-buyer-experience-report/); [CustomerThink](https://customerthink.com/research-round-up-6sense-study-provides-critical-insights-on-b2b-buyer-behavior/); [BusinessWire, Nov 12 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251112018032/en)
- **G2 2025 Buyer Behavior Report (1,169 B2B decision-makers, April 2025):** GenAI chatbots (**17%**) and review sites (**15%**) were the top two shortlist influences, ahead of vendor sites and analyst firms. 29% started research with a chatbot more often than Google. — [G2 Research](https://research.g2.com/cmos-2025-buyer-behavior-report-research-g2); [G2 newsroom](https://company.g2.com/news/buyer-behavior-in-2025)
- **G2 follow-up (March 2026, 1,076 buyers):** **51%** start research with an AI chatbot more often than Google, and **71%** use chatbots somewhere in software research. Chatbots are the top shortlist influence at **54%**, ahead of review sites at **43%**. ChatGPT dominates (63%). The top chatbot use case is comparing vendor strengths and weaknesses (41%). (The 17% and 54% figures come from different survey designs and are not directly comparable.) — [G2 Learn](https://learn.g2.com/ai-search-surging-for-b2b-buyers); [G2 2026 AI Search Insight Report](https://learn.g2.com/g2-2026-ai-search-insight-report?tp=1)
- **Gartner rep-free preference:** **61%** prefer a rep-free buying experience (June 25 2025 release; 632 buyers surveyed Aug–Sept 2024). This rose to **67%** in the March 9 2026 release (646 buyers, Aug–Sept 2025), and **45%** used AI during a recent purchase. — [Gartner 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience); [Gartner 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience)
- **Gartner, May 20 2026:** 69% of buyers prefer to validate AI-generated insights with sales reps. — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-20-gartner-survey-finds-sixty-nine-percent-of-b-two-b-buyers-turn-to-sales-reps-to-validate-ai-generated-insights)
- **TrustRadius 2025:** **72%** of buyers saw Google AI Overviews during research, and **90%** of those clicked through to a cited source. — [Digital Commerce 360, Apr 16 2025](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/04/16/ai-disrupts-b2b-buying-trust-drives-tech-purchase-decisions/). TrustRadius 2026: 94% fact-check AI, 74% use reviews (see §2). — [Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/news-brief/trustradius-ai-has-changed-how-buyers-research-but-not-what-they-trust/53853/)
- **Edelman–LinkedIn 2025:** thought leadership reaches "hidden" buyers outside the formal buying group. 55% of decision-makers reportedly use thought leadership to vet vendors (attribution inconsistent). — [Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/news-brief/marketers-need-to-gear-content-to-hidden-b2b-buyers-edelman-linkedin/49901/)

### Inferences
- With 95% of winners on the day-one list and AI chatbots as the top shortlist influence, the job of content is (a) to be remembered **before** a need arises, and (b) to be the raw material AI assistants and peers draw on when they recommend vendors. Generic "how-to" posts do neither: they don't build memory, and AI doesn't need to cite them.
- The "verify" layer (reviews, peers, reps, cited sources: 90% click through) is where trust is actually earned. Customer evidence and third-party mentions have more weight than vendor-owned prose.

### Gaps
- No Forrester 2025–2026 buyer-study figures were retrieved in this pass.
- No data found on the role of communities (Slack groups, Reddit) in shortlisting specifically, beyond the TrustRadius Gen Z social-proof finding.

## 5. Formats hard to fake with AI

### Takeaway
No study ranks formats by "AI-fakeability." But the evidence above points to formats that carry **verifiable, accountable, first-hand information**: original data that exists nowhere else, named customer voices and reviews, a named human taking a position, provenance-signed media, and live interaction with a human (reps, events). These match what buyers verify against and what readers do not penalize.

### Cited Findings
- AI-written articles mostly don't surface in Google or ChatGPT results (Graphite), which suggests distribution systems already discount commodity text. — [GPTZero](https://gptzero.me/news/ai-written-web/)
- AI recommendations are built from third-party content: reviews, independent publications, peer accounts (TrustRadius 2026). Being *in* that corpus with unique facts is what matters. — [Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/news-brief/trustradius-ai-has-changed-how-buyers-research-but-not-what-they-trust/53853/)
- Customer voices: 74% of buyers use reviews (TrustRadius 2026). Review sites are the #2 shortlist influence (G2 2026: 43%). — [Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/industry-news/news-brief/trustradius-ai-has-changed-how-buyers-research-but-not-what-they-trust/53853/); [G2](https://learn.g2.com/ai-search-surging-for-b2b-buyers)
- Live human validation: 69% of buyers want a rep to validate AI insights (Gartner 2026). — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-20-gartner-survey-finds-sixty-nine-percent-of-b-two-b-buyers-turn-to-sales-reps-to-validate-ai-generated-insights)
- Provenance: C2PA labels raise trust in experiments, but comprehension is weak. — [ICWSM](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/42749); [CEUR-WS](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4056/short4.pdf)
- Human-made premium: Thompson argues provenance makes human work more valuable as AI work proliferates (paraphrase). — [Stratechery 2026](https://stratechery.com/2026/ai-and-the-human-condition/)
- In marketing/branding on LinkedIn, human-written posts outperformed AI posts by 73% (Originality.ai 2025). — [Originality.ai](https://originality.ai/blog/linkedin-ai-study-engagement)

### Inferences (for a B2B agency founder whose content "looks like everyone else's")
- **Proprietary data:** use TripleDart client-portfolio benchmarks and Slate usage data. AI cannot generate numbers it has never seen, and this is exactly the "unique fact" that AI assistants cite.
- **Founder's real numbers / building in public:** these are tied to a named, accountable person. They are hard to fake, and they fit the Edelman finding that trust is concentrating in close circles.
- **Opinionated predictions with a track record:** these build reputation over time, because a scored record can't be produced on demand. (Inference; no direct study found.)
- **Named customer voices** (video and reviews on G2/TrustRadius): these feed both the human verification layer and the AI recommendation corpus.
- **Live formats** (events, office hours, unscripted calls): these line up with buyers' wish to validate with a human. (Inference; no direct study found.)
- **Avoid the AI "look":** per Raptive, *suspected* AI halves trust regardless of authorship. Templated hooks, listicle structure, and stock-feeling AI images carry a cost even when a human wrote the content.
